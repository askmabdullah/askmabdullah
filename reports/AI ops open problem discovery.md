# Sell checked monthly work, not AI tools

**Bet on back-office jobs that run to a deadline, not on real-time AI.** The three best opportunities for Wanile are: (1) a monthly multi-entity close for small US private-equity roll-ups and first-time business buyers; (2) a client due-diligence desk for Australian law, accounting, conveyancing and real estate firms caught by the new AML/CTF "Tranche 2" rules; and (3) a second-look underpayment and aged-denial recovery service for US medical practices, sold white-label through small billing companies. All three passed every one of your six rules. Buyers already pay people to do this work, and your team can do it during the Lahore day and hand it back for a US or Australian morning. Several ideas that look exciting got weaker under a closer look. A funded startup already runs the "AI plus offshore humans" model for CPG deductions. Platforms now give away fax intake. Order-entry software costs about $1 to $2 an order. Payment-fraud tools ship with $1 million loss guarantees. Real-time work (same-hour quotes, live callbacks, patient intake) would put your team on night shifts, and US law keeps customs records onshore. Treat every number below as a lead, not a fact: almost nothing was read on the full page, so the 14-day tests in Step 5 should settle the final order.

### Card 1. CPG retailer deductions (Step 1 #9)

**Real money, paid for today and easy to run offshore. But a funded startup already runs your exact model for the long tail. Weaker after the deep dive.**

1. **The task.** Every week someone logs into each retailer and distributor portal (Walmart Retail Link, Amazon Vendor Central, KeHE Connect, UNFI). They pull each short-payment and match it to the PO, invoice, bill of lading, proof of delivery and promotion agreement. If the deduction is wrong, they file a dispute with evidence before the deadline. Then they chase the money and post it. UNFI codes each deduction by family, such as 01 for shortages and 05 for damages ([Endless Commerce](https://endlesscommerce.com/playbook/kehe-and-unfi-deductions-codes-and-clocks/), vendor).
2. **Who feels it, who pays.** A deductions or accounts-receivable specialist feels it, often part-time at small brands. The founder or CEO signs below about $10M revenue. Above that, the controller, VP Finance or a fractional CFO signs. SPS even publishes an ROI calculator to "justify deduction management tools to your CFO" ([SPS](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/justify-deduction-management-tools-roi-calculator)).
3. **How often, how many hours.** Weekly, with every payment. One small brand budgets 15-20 hours a week for UNFI and KeHE deductions alone ([Djinni job post](https://djinni.co/jobs/831947-distributor-deductions-and-chargeback-special/), seen October 2026). Estimate for a $20M brand: 2-5% of revenue deducted is $400K-$1M a year. At an assumed $300-$1,000 per deduction, that is 12-40 deductions a week. At 30-45 minutes each, that is 6-30 hours a week. Speed matters: recovery falls from 80% to 20% as disputes near 90 days ([HighRadius](https://www.highradius.com/resources/case-studies/kraft-heinz-dms/), vendor claim).
4. **What it costs a month.** Estimate for a $20M brand: $50K-$83K is deducted each month. If 10-25% of that is invalid (vendor range), then $5K-$21K a month is lost. Labour adds $1.9K-$2.7K (15-20 hours × $22-$24 an hour × 1.3 overhead × 4.33 weeks). A $5M brand loses only about $0.8K-$5.2K a month. A hypothetical 20-25% contingency fee, on 60-75% of invalid dollars won back, yields about $600-$3.9K a month per $20M client (estimate).
5. **How it is handled today.** In-house or part-time staff at $45K-$50K a year ([C.A. Fortune post](https://builtincharlotte.com/job/deductions-specialist/8318357)). Software: SupplyPike at about $810 a month for suppliers under $10M of Walmart sales (single aggregator, [SoftwareSuggest](https://www.softwaresuggest.com/supplypike)); Promomash at $349-$1,250 a month ([Capterra UK](https://www.capterra.co.uk/software/210477/promomash)). Glimpse charges a small monthly fee plus commission. Carbon6 charges only on money recovered. No published contingency percentage was found.
6. **Proof.** TeaSquares invoiced KeHE $32,000 and was paid $21,000 after deductions and fees. The founder: "many of them are erroneously charged to brands, and it's up to them to contest it" ([Foodbevy guide](https://guide.foodbevy.com/?p=1150), exact page not confirmed). The average Walmart supplier loses 5.8% of invoices to deductions ([SupplyPike](https://supplypike.com/walmart), vendor claim). Glimpse is hiring Philippines staff "to act as the expert human-in-the-loop behind our AI-powered deductions platform" ([Indeed Philippines](https://ph.indeed.com/q-glimpse-jobs.html)). No forum quotes were reachable.
7. **Who else sells a fix.** Glimpse ($10M Series A, April 2025; 140 to 200+ brands; KeHE and UNFI focus) ([Food Industry Executive](https://foodindustryexecutive.com/2025/04/glimpse-secures-10m-to-automate-deduction-management-for-cpg-brands/)). Also SPS/SupplyPike, Carbon6, iNymbus, Inmar, Promomash, Vividly, HighRadius, ARPflow (170+ clients in India) and BELAY. Crowding: high.
8. **Next 24 months.** The problem stays the same size, because retailer policy drives it, not AI. The service margin shrinks, because vendors are automating triage, evidence matching and portal filing. Expect 12-24 months before the long tail is fully productised (inference).
9. **How to reach buyers.** Expo West (about 3,200 exhibitors and 66,000 registrants in 2026, [New Hope](https://www.newhope.com/natural-products-expos/natural-products-expo-west-2026-numbers-data-certifications)), Foodbevy, BevNET, food brokers such as PRESENCE, Settle's partner directory, CPG fractional CFOs and Walmart supplier educators (8th & Walton). Glimpse already works most of these.
10. **How many US companies.** Estimate: 3,000-6,000 brands of $2M-$100M selling through distributors or big-box retailers, possibly double with direct suppliers and non-food CPG. Inputs: UNFI has about 10,000-11,000 suppliers ([UNFI 2025 Impact Report](https://s205.q4cdn.com/522321978/files/doc_financials/2025/sr/2025_UNFI_Impact_Report.pdf)) and KeHE about 4,100 ([KeHE](https://kehe.com/brands)). Heavy overlap is assumed.
11. **Strongest argument against.** Glimpse already sells AI plus offshore human review, to this exact long tail, through these exact channels. What is left (regional grocers, club stores, non-food CPG, root-cause prevention) leaves a thin fee from small brands.

### Card 2. Customs entry prep and tariff refund work (Step 1 #19)

**Constant rule changes make this painful, and brokers are hiring. But US law keeps the records and the sign-off onshore, and the refund windfall fades by late 2027. Stronger on pain, weaker on fit.**

1. **The task.** For each shipment, an entry writer opens the supplier invoice and packing list. They match each line to a 10-digit tariff (HTS) code, check country of origin and which extra tariffs apply, compute values, key the entry into filing software (CargoWise, Descartes, Magaya) and fix rejections. Since 24 July 2026 that includes new Section 301 "forced labor" tariffs of 10% or 12.5% on imports from 60 economies, with Section 232 goods carved out ([Thomson Reuters](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/help/onesource-global-trade/regulatory-insights/2026/july-24th/us-imposes-section-301-forced-labor-tariffs-60.html)). Side work: IEEPA refund declarations in CBP's CAPE portal, and keeping importer records accurate, because CBP began voiding bad importer numbers on 18 September 2026 ([Federal Register](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-08-19/html/2026-16911.htm)). A licensed broker signs off.
2. **Who feels it, who pays.** Entry writers and licensed brokers feel it. The owner or licensed principal signs, because their licence carries a duty of "responsible supervision and control" ([19 CFR 111.28](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-19/chapter-I/part-111/subpart-C/section-111.28)). For refund work at importers, the controller or CFO signs.
3. **How often, how many hours.** Every shipment. Estimate: a small active broker filing 100 entries a week, at 15-45 minutes each, spends 25-75 staff hours a week (0.6-1.9 people). The average firm files only about 28 entries a week (9M entries ÷ 6,200 firms; both inputs unconfirmed), so volume is skewed to big brokers. Each tariff change adds a re-check burst of about 33 hours for a client with 2,000 products (estimate at 1 minute per product).
4. **What it costs a month.** Estimate: $65K midpoint pay × 1.3 ÷ 2,080 hours = $40.60 an hour. So 25-75 hours a week costs $4,400-$13,200 a month. That is 7-20% of a 100-entry broker's fee revenue (100 × 4.33 × an assumed $150 fee ≈ $65K a month). Entry writers are posted at $55K-$80K ([ZipRecruiter](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Customs-Brokerage-Entry-Writer/3)).
5. **How it is handled today.** In-house entry writers, filing software, extraction add-ons that feed CargoWise (Expedock, Cargo Docket), and refund firms charging 3-28% contingency, with about 5% the loose broker norm ([cape-guide](https://cape-guide.pages.dev/blog/lawyer-broker-recovery-firm-who-files-ieepa-refund/), vendor).
6. **Proof.** The Supreme Court struck down IEEPA tariffs on 20 February 2026 ([ABA](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/public_contract_law/resources/procurement-lawyer/2026-fall/ieepa-tariffs-struck-down-replacement-tariffs-expired/)). By mid-September 2026, CAPE had about 286,000 declarations, $134.7B of refunds accepted and about $122B (with interest) sent for payment ([C.H. Robinson, 17 Sep 2026](https://www.chrobinson.com/en-au/resources/insights-and-advisories/client-advisories/2026q3/09-17-26-cbp-cape-phase-3-ieepa-refunds/)). A Seko Logistics brokerage director worried "whether the added staff is versed in this extremely complicated environment" ([The Loadstar](https://theloadstar.com/tariff-turmoil-sees-cpb-recruit-retirees-to-deal-with-the-havoc/), attribution inferred). Prosimo.io posted an entry-writer job at up to $75K on 9 October 2026 ([job board](https://jobs.generalcatalyst.com/companies/prosimo-io-2/jobs/96375269-customs-entry-writer)).
7. **Who else sells a fix.** CargoWise now parses invoices with AI inside its own system ([viago](https://viago-website.pages.dev/cargowise-ai-features.html), vendor-adjacent). Gaia Dynamics has a $7M seed and claims 92% classification accuracy ([Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/gaia-dynamics-raises-7-million-seed-round-led-by-corazon-capital)). Also Amari ($4.5M), Alchemize (a YC AI-native brokerage), Raft, Camtom and Zonos. Software crowding is high. Nobody was found selling a done-for-you, broker-supervised prep desk for small brokers.
8. **Next 24 months.** Raw keying shrinks as filing software ships AI. Refund work tails off between mid and late 2027 (inference from liquidation and protest timelines the notes mark unverified). Tariff churn has no end date found, so licensed review and exception work stay.
9. **How to reach buyers.** NCBFAA and its annual conference ([2025 exhibitors](https://ncbfaa.org/membership-benefits/conferences-events/annual-conference-2025/exhibitors/current-exhibitors-and-floorplan/gaia-dynamics)), regional broker associations (names not re-checked), CBP's CSMS email bulletins, CargoWise and Descartes partner ecosystems, The Loadstar and JOC.
10. **How many US companies.** 14,454 licensed individual brokers ([CBP](https://cbp.gov/trade/programs-administration/customs-brokers/becoming-customs-broker)) and about 6,200 brokerage firms ([Camtom](https://www.camtomx.com/en/blog/what-is-a-customs-broker), vendor estimate). Estimate: 4,000-5,000 are small firms under 50 staff.
11. **Strongest argument against.** The buyer is a thin-margin service business whose software vendor now bundles extraction. Original records, including electronic ones, must stay inside US customs territory ([19 CFR 111.23](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/19/111.23)). And the refund money that would fund early sales is mostly processed already.

### Card 3. RFQ and quote turnaround (Step 1 #34)

**The biggest dollar prize on the list, because a slow quote loses the whole job. But winning depends on answering within hours, in US daytime, and owners guard their pricing.**

1. **The task.** At a distributor, an inside sales rep opens an emailed request for quote with 5 to 200 lines. They match each part to a SKU or substitute, check stock and lead times, ask the manufacturer for special pricing on big lines, apply the customer's price level, set the margin (a manager approves anything below the floor), build the quote in the ERP and email it back, ideally within an hour. At a job shop, an estimator reads the drawing (material, tolerances, finish), plans the routing, estimates setup and machine time, gets outside quotes, and the owner sets the price.
2. **Who feels it, who pays.** Inside sales reps and estimators feel it. The VP Sales or inside sales manager signs at distributors; the owner or general manager signs at job shops. At firms under $100M, the president often signs both this and order entry.
3. **How often, how many hours.** Daily. Estimate for a mid-size distributor with 150 RFQs a week: 70% simple (10 minutes), 25% medium (45 minutes), 5% project bids (4 hours) gives about 30 minutes average, so 75 hours a week (1.9 people). A busy job shop handling 80 RFQs a week at 45-90 minutes each spends 60-120 estimator hours a week ([Leanware](https://leanware.co/insights/rfq-automation-job-shops-mgas), vendor estimate).
4. **What it costs a month.** Labour estimate: 75 hours × $29-$48 an hour × 4.33 = $9.4K-$15.6K a month at a distributor; $2.9K-$26K at a job shop. Lost-deal estimate (all inputs assumed): if same-day quoting adds 5 points of win rate on 150 RFQs a week worth $3,000 each, that is 7.5 more wins a week, about $97K a month in revenue, or about $21K in gross profit at a 22% margin. A small job shop gains about $6.5K a month in contribution on the same logic.
5. **How it is handled today.** In-house staff. Quoting software: general CPQ at $10-$150 per user per month; job-shop tools such as Uptool from $195 a month and Paperless Parts (its $300 and $750 tiers date from 2019). Some shops lean on marketplaces such as Xometry, which kept a 35.7% marketplace gross margin in Q3 2025 ([Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/35886037/xometry-reports-record-third-quarter-2025-results)).
6. **Proof.** The chief commercial officer of a national industrial distributor: "If you respond within an hour, your probability of getting the business is higher… It takes two hours, it drops." Their median response was three to four hours ([Instalily case study](https://instalily.ai/case-studies/quoting-speed), vendor-hosted, undated). Early Paperless Parts data: shops quoting within two hours win 90%+, shops taking five days win under 5% ([Docket](https://www.docket.io/blog/speed-to-quote-is-the-1-win-rate-variable-in-manufacturing-sales), vendor, secondhand). Job posts in 2026: electrical-distributor inside sales at $47K-$57K plus $9K-$12K commission ([Insight Global](https://jobs.insightglobal.com/find_a_job/virginia/job-486748)); a CNC machinist-estimator at $80K (posted 5 May 2026, [career.com](https://www.career.com/job/cnc-machine-shop/cnc-machinist-estimator/j202605050702057048470)). All win-rate data is vendor data.
7. **Who else sells a fix.** Instalily has raised nearly $100M, including a $60M Series B in July 2026, and serves large distributors ([MDM](https://www.mdm.com/news/technology/ai/instalily-raises-60m-launches-ai-engineer-for-enterprise-operations/)). Also Proton.ai, Endeavor ($7M seed), Canals, and job-shop tools (Paperless Parts, AMFG, DigiFabster, CloudNC). Crowded at the top; distributors under about $100M are thinly served.
8. **Next 24 months.** Holds. Better AI makes line matching and stock checks near-automatic. Margin decisions, substitutes, special pricing and drawing-based cost judgement keep a human accountable. Buyers' own AI may send more RFQs to more suppliers, which raises the premium on speed (inference).
9. **How to reach buyers.** ERP resellers and consultants for Prophet 21, Eclipse, Sage 100, Acumatica and NetSuite; ISA (145 distributor members) and NAED; NTMA for job shops; MDM, Distribution Strategy Group and the Electrical Wholesaling podcast.
10. **How many US companies.** Estimate: 12,000-20,000 target distributors in electrical, plumbing/HVAC, industrial and building products (assumption-driven, from an old 2002 Census base and NAW's 250,000 North American count). Machine shops: about 16,600-18,000 ([IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/number-of-businesses/machine-shop-services/645/)).
11. **Strongest argument against.** Price is the owner's most guarded decision, so your team can only prepare the quote. Value shows up as win rate, which is hard to prove and slow to sell. Defense drawings are off-limits to foreign persons under ITAR ([Cofactr](https://www.cofactr.com/articles/a-practical-guide-to-itar-compliance-for-manufacturers-and-engineers)).

### Card 4. Emailed PO order entry into the ERP (Step 1 #8)

**The easiest return to prove, because the labour sits on payroll. Also the fastest-commoditising problem on the list: software now costs about $1 to $2 an order. Weaker after the deep dive.**

1. **The task.** A customer-service rep opens a PO (PDF, fax, spreadsheet or voicemail), finds the account and ship-to, maps the customer's part numbers to internal SKUs, checks price against the quote or contract, checks stock and ship date, saves the sales order, sends an acknowledgement and calls about any mismatch.
2. **Who feels it, who pays.** Order-entry staff and their supervisor feel it. The VP Operations, COO, CFO or owner signs.
3. **How often, how many hours.** Daily. Vendors put manual entry at 6 minutes ([WorkD](https://www.workd.com/insights/articles/automating-order-entry/)) to 20 minutes per order ([WizCommerce](https://wizcommerce.com/product/ai-order-entry-software/)). Estimate: 300 orders a week × 6-20 minutes = 30-100 hours a week (0.75-2.5 people).
4. **What it costs a month.** Estimate: labour of 30-100 hours × $21-$34 an hour × 4.33 = $2.7K-$14.7K a month. Errors: 1,300 orders × 1-3% error rate × about $250 per bad order = $3.3K-$9.8K a month (vendor inputs from Conexiom's CEO). Total about $6K-$24K a month.
5. **How it is handled today.** In-house reps; staffing agencies supply order-entry temps at $19-$26 an hour (for example [Robert Half, $23-$26](https://jobs.hireheroesusa.org/jobs/558691601-order-entry-specialist-at-robert-half)); offshore data entry at $5-$12 an hour; EDI for big customers only.
6. **Proof.** "Seven out of ten business-to-business orders still move through some combination of email, phone calls, fax machines, and spreadsheets" (Conexiom data via [Workd, 2026](https://www.workd.com/insights/articles/hidden-cost-managing-b2b-orders-email-2026/), vendor claim). A mid-size distributor executive calls order entry the company's top AI use ([Distribution Strategy archive](https://archive.distributionstrategy.com/?p=6067), date unconfirmed). A Conexiom user review: "A bit pricey but understandable, as it does save a lot of labor costs" ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/205134/Conexiom/reviews/2425333/), undated).
7. **Who else sells a fix.** At least 15 vendors: Conexiom (now in Epicor's partner directory), Y Meadows (its own ROI calculator assumes $1.50 per order), WizCommerce Ella (connects to QuickBooks), Comena (YC S25), OrderScan from $100, OrderSync from $1,000 a month, Pepper ($50M, foodservice), Anchr, Burnt, Proton, Endeavor, Canals and Esker. Very crowded.
8. **Next 24 months.** Shrinks as a paid service. Vendors claim labour per order falls 70-90%. What stays human: unknown part numbers, price disputes, phone orders, and old ERPs with no API.
9. **How to reach buyers.** ERP resellers and partner directories (Epicor, Acumatica Marketplace, NetSuite), NAW, ISA, NAED and MDM. The same buyer often owns quoting, so this pairs with Card 3.
10. **How many US companies.** Estimate: 24,000-39,000 US durable-goods wholesalers with 20-499 staff (assumption-driven), of which about 12,000-20,000 sit in the target sub-sectors.
11. **Strongest argument against.** A buyer can now get software at $1-$2 an order. A service must win on guaranteed accuracy and on posting into legacy ERPs, not on keying.

### Card 5. Fax and email referral intake (Step 1 #21)

**Weaker after the deep dive. The software buyers already use now reads the fax for them, and cheap tools handle 200 referrals for $549 a month. What is left is exception chasing during US business hours.**

1. **The task.** An intake coordinator reads a 10 to 40 page fax. They find the order, face sheet, insurance card and clinical notes, create the patient and order in the system, run eligibility, check the payer's paperwork rules, and fax or call the referring office for the missing signature or notes.
2. **Who feels it, who pays.** Intake coordinators feel it. The DME owner or general manager, the home health administrator, or the practice administrator signs.
3. **How often, how many hours.** Daily. Estimate for a DME supplier with 150 orders a week: 24 minutes of keying per order (from Tennr's "under 100 orders per person per week" manual benchmark, vendor) gives 60 hours. Chasing 40% incomplete orders at 15 minutes each adds 15 hours. Total about 75 hours a week (1.9 people). A 4-physician specialty practice with 60 referrals a week at about 37.5 minutes each needs about 37.5 hours a week.
4. **What it costs a month.** Estimate: DME labour $6.3K-$9.0K a month; specialty practice $3.2K-$4.5K; about $7.80-$11.04 of labour per referral before chasing. Lost referrals could add $10K-$21K a month, but that rests on an unverified "$800 per lost referral" figure from vendor blogs.
5. **How it is handled today.** In-house staff at $15-$27 an hour (ZipRecruiter average $21.23, [June 2026](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Home-Health-Intake-Coordinator/2)); offshore assistants at $5-$12 an hour; DME billing companies with intake teams; software such as Medsender at $299, $549 (200 referrals) and $2,249 a month ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/215989/Medsender/pricing/)).
6. **Proof.** 35% of inbound healthcare documents are still faxes, 52% of faxes need manual handling, and 88% of practitioners say fax delays affect care (vendor survey, October 2025, [Documo](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251015114710/en/88-of-Healthcare-Practitioners-Say-Fax-Related-Delays-Impact-Patient-Care-New-Survey-Finds)). About 75% of US medical institutions still rely on fax ([JMIR 2025](https://medinform.jmir.org/2025/1/e62983)). Tennr says referrals "often requir[e] hours of manual review and back-and-forth communication" ([HIT Consultant](https://hitconsultant.net/2025/06/18/tennr-raises-101m-to-solve-healthcares-patient-referral-black-hole/)). No small-practice manager quotes were found.
7. **Who else sells a fix.** Brightree (DME software) now reads and classifies referral faxes and creates orders ([Brightree](https://www.brightree.com/brightree-intelligent-document-automation/)). WellSky added AI intake in January 2026 ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260127232204/en/WellSky-Launches-New-AI-Capabilities-to-Automate-and-Scale-Referral-Intake)). Tennr raised $101M at a $605M valuation ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/06/18/tennr-health-tech-ai-patient-referral-ivp-a16z-lightspeed-iconiq-series-c/)). Also Medsender, Assort Health and Darby. Platform-layer crowding: high.
8. **Next 24 months.** Shrinks. "Fax to fields" will be near free inside the main DME and home health platforms. Fax itself persists, because the new CMS rule targets payer APIs, not referrals between providers. Medicare's new WISeR prior-auth model in six states adds paperwork for some DME items (inference).
9. **How to reach buyers.** HME News, Medtrade, AAHomecare, NAHC and state home care associations, DME billing companies, and Brightree and WellSky partner ecosystems.
10. **How many US companies.** Not pinned down. The notes estimate 60,000-70,000 independent physician practices, built on AMA's 42.2% private-practice share ([AMA](https://www.ama-assn.org/about/research/physician-practice-benchmark-survey)) and an unverified physician base. DME supplier and home health counts were not retrieved.
11. **Strongest argument against.** The platforms are bundling the core job, the leftover work is time-critical in US hours, and patient data rules limit offshore access (see Step 4).

### Card 6. Denials and underpayments at independent practices (Step 1 #2)

**The best-evidenced pain in healthcare, and the most crowded market. It survives as a narrow wedge: a second-look audit of underpayments and aged denials, sold through billing companies.**

1. **The task.** A denial specialist opens the day's remittance files. They sort denials by reason (eligibility, authorisation, coding, medical necessity, timely filing), fix and resubmit the easy ones, pull records and write appeals for the rest, and chase status. Once a month they compare what each payer paid with the contracted rate to catch short payments. Underpayments "hide inside zero-balance claims", so they look paid ([PayerPrice](https://payerprice.com/blog/underpayment-detection), vendor).
2. **Who feels it, who pays.** Billing managers, denial specialists and AR callers feel it. The practice administrator, owner physician or CFO signs, or the owner of the billing company.
3. **How often, how many hours.** Daily. Estimate for a 5-physician practice: 500 claims a week; at 8-10% denials that is 173-217 denials a month. At 30 minutes each that is 108 hours, plus about 36 hours of status checks, so about 145 hours a month (0.85 of a person). A billing company with 30 such clients carries about 25 people's worth of this work.
4. **What it costs a month.** Estimate (assumes $120 paid per claim, so about $260K collected a month): $21K-$26K is denied each month. If 50-65% of denials are never worked (vendor figure) and 54% of those were winnable (Premier, 2022 hospital claims, published 2024), $7K-$9K a month is left behind. Underpayments at 1-5% of collections add $2.6K-$13K. Total leak about $9.6K-$22K a month. At 25% contingency that is up to $2.4K-$5.5K a month per practice, if all of it is recovered.
5. **How it is handled today.** Full-service billing companies at 4-10% of collections, with 5-8% typical for small practices ([Neolytix](https://neolytix.com/articles/what-is-the-going-rate-for-medical-billing-services/), vendor guide); in-house billers; offshore teams at $5-$12 an hour; contingency denial firms at 25% ([Relvio](https://relvio-health.pages.dev/), vendor).
6. **Proof.** In an MGMA poll on 6 January 2026 (288 responses), 48% named "denials and appeals" the biggest leak in their revenue cycle ([Physicians Practice](https://www.physicianspractice.com/view/mgma-poll-denials-are-biggest-revenue-cycle-leak-for-medical-practices)). Experian's 2025 survey found 41% of providers with denial rates of 10% or more, 43% understaffed, and about 90% of denials reworked by humans ([Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/providers/experian-study-providers-say-claims-denials-continue-rise)). Surgery-centre leaders call payers "more aggressive, automated and less collaborative" ([Becker's ASC](https://www.beckersasc.com/?p=98908)). Job posts: remote denial specialists at $18-$22 an hour ([BrightSpring](https://careers-brightspring.icims.com/jobs/192000/denial-management-specialist---remote/job)) and $25-$28 (posted 17 January 2026, [AnswersNow](https://jobs.techstars.com/companies/getanswersnow-com/jobs/65416447-accounts-receivable-denials-specialist)).
7. **Who else sells a fix.** About 2,000 billing companies (HBMA, 2011 estimate, older). Commure/Athelas sells full billing to independent ortho, ENT, podiatry and therapy practices ([Athelas](https://www.athelas.com/case-studies/south-valley-ent)). athenahealth launched denial automation on 3 June 2026 ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260603429867/en/)); WebPT launched AI billing for therapy in July 2026. Small tools: Fight Paperwork at $25 a month plus $10 per appeal; NoNos and Relvio at 25% contingency. Services crowding: very high. Underpayment audits at small practices: medium.
8. **Next 24 months.** Grows. Payers are automating denials (Medicare's WISeR model started January 2026 in six states, [Georgetown CHIR](https://medicare.chir.georgetown.edu/new-cms-wiser-model-revives-concerns-of-prior-authorization-and-artificial-intelligence/)). Practice software absorbs simple coding denials first, which leaves more complex appeals and contract underpayments.
9. **How to reach buyers.** HBMA, with nearly 500 member billing firms ([HBMA](https://hbma.org/content/about/about-hbma)) whose typical member had 40-50 staff (older document); AAPC forums and chapters; MGMA state chapters; APTA Private Practice; athenahealth and WebPT marketplaces; Physicians Practice, Medical Economics and Becker's newsletters.
10. **How many US companies.** 42.2% of US physicians were in physician-owned practice in 2024 ([AMA](https://www.ama-assn.org/about/research/physician-practice-benchmark-survey)); ophthalmology (70.4%) and orthopedics (54%) lead. The notes' order-of-magnitude estimate is 60,000-70,000 independent practices, built on an unverified physician base. Plus about 2,000 billing companies.
11. **Strongest argument against.** Every target already pays someone to do denials. EHR vendors and Athelas are moving into the same small-practice segment. Payer and state Medicaid contracts can block offshore access to patient data.

### Card 7. Multi-entity monthly close for roll-ups and first-time owners (Step 1 #57)

**Still strong after the deep dive. Buyers pay $120K to $175K for a controller to do this, the work runs to a monthly deadline, and nobody was found selling it as an AI-plus-human service for small roll-ups.**

1. **The task.** Each month, pull 10 to 30 QuickBooks or Xero trial balances. Map each to the group chart of accounts. Fix miscoded items. Book accruals, intercompany charges and eliminations. Consolidate. Write site benchmarks and variance comments for the sponsor's pack, typically due 15-20 business days after month-end, with some sponsors pushing for 10 ([Madras Accountancy](https://madrasaccountancy.com/blog-posts/outsourced-financial-reporting-private-equity-portfolio), vendor). Each new add-on needs an opening balance sheet, a cash-to-accrual conversion and a mapping. A first-time owner (search fund or SBA buyer) needs the one-company version: an accrual close plus a monthly investor update.
2. **Who feels it, who pays.** The platform controller or VP Finance feels it, and so do the site managers being chased for data. The platform CFO or CEO signs, with strong influence from the sponsor's operating partner. For search-fund and SBA buyers, the owner-CEO feels it and signs.
3. **How often, how many hours.** Monthly, plus a one-time onboarding per add-on. Estimate for 14 entities: 4-8 hours per entity plus 15-30 hours of group work = 71-142 hours a month (0.45-0.9 of a person). For 30 entities: 140-280 hours. Each add-on adds 20-60 one-time hours. A first-time owner needs about 19-38 hours a month. No sourced benchmark exists; these are estimates to test in interviews.
4. **What it costs a month.** Estimate: a multi-entity controller at $120K-$175K base × 1.3 ÷ 1,920 hours = $81-$119 an hour, so 71-142 hours costs $5.8K-$16.9K a month. A full-time hire runs about $19K a month ($160K × 1.15 bonus × 1.25 benefits ÷ 12). A wrong number is costly: a $100K EBITDA error at a 9x exit multiple is about $900K of enterprise value (illustration, not a sourced loss).
5. **How it is handled today.** A holding-company controller; fractional CFOs at $6K-$25K a month ([Acuity](https://acuity.co/fractional-cfo/), vendor); outsourced multi-entity bookkeeping at $1.5K-$4K+ a month ([Ecosire](https://ecosire.com/blog/outsourced-bookkeeping-cost-2026), vendor); bookkeeping on SearchFunder from $450 a month ([SearchFunder](https://searchfunder.com/post/bookkeepers-please-introduce-yourself-here)). Software: Fathom ($53-$720 a month) and Reach ($149-$950) consolidate, but Fathom requires each entity to be imported first ([Fathom support](https://support.fathomhq.com/en/articles/999174)). The clean-up is left to people.
6. **Proof.** A lower-middle-market PE firm with about a dozen operating companies posted a holding-company controller role at up to $160K plus 10-20% bonus on 14 January 2026 ([PCRecruiter](https://host.pcrecruiter.net/pcrbin/jobboard/job/Controller/b/LAD7CGTS6ZMHSILH2XDSJU6XWBWSXRIDZTODGWAFRETOKJZZM7MA2LMN3SG5LUM3MWJVATBIU4), URL match likely). Other 2026 multi-entity controller posts ran $120K-$175K, including one at $145K-$170K for 10-15 legal entities ([Mediabistro listings](https://www.mediabistro.com/jobs/3523131918-controller); salary-to-page match not confirmed). A roll-up guide: "AI does not clean the data, and that is where most roll-up finance pain lives" ([WorkWise](https://workwisesolutions.org/guides/best-ai-roll-up-consolidation-software.html), guide, not an operator). A SearchFunder post: "cash basis of accounting made it difficult for the PE firm to track profitability and report to LPs" ([SearchFunder](https://searchfunder.com/post/from-cash-flow-to-clearer-financial-picture-helping-client-thrive), vendor-authored). No operator podcast quotes were reachable.
7. **Who else sells a fix.** AI accounting roll-ups: Crete plans to spend $500M+ buying US accounting firms ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/thrive-backed-accounting-firm-crete-130200467.html)); Modus ($85M) and Multiplier ($62.5M). AI-native ERPs: Rillet (about $20K-$35K a year), DualEntry, Campfire. Offshore F&A firms already pitch PE portfolios (Personiv, QX). Startup bookkeepers (Pilot, Kruze, indinero). No firm was found selling an AI-plus-human multi-entity close for roll-ups specifically. Crowding: high and rising.
8. **Next 24 months.** The tool gap shrinks, but the need for a checked, signed close holds. There were 13,509 US PE-backed companies at the end of Q2 2026 ([Penn Mutual AM](https://www.pennmutualam.com/market-insights/blogs/chart-of-the-week/2026-10-22-private-equitys-unsold-backlog-keeps-growing-as-exit-routes-narrow)), and add-ons were 74.9% of US buyouts through Q3 2026 ([CLA on PitchBook](https://www.claconnect.com/en/resources/blogs/private-equity/larger-deals-lift-us-private-equity-but-selectivity-still-matters)). Expect price per entity to fall as AI makes mapping cheaper for everyone.
9. **How to reach buyers.** ACG (58-61 chapters, 13,000-14,500 members, [ACG](https://www.acg.org/101/membership-tools/join-or-renew)); SearchFunder; SBA acquisition lenders (Live Oak, Huntington, First Internet); Intuit ProAdvisor, Fathom and Reach partner directories; operating partners; CPA Trendlines and Capstone deal trackers.
10. **How many US companies.** Estimate: 1,700-2,700 PE platforms with 5+ entities still on mixed ledgers (assumes 25-40% of 13,509 are platforms and half are on mixed ledgers). Plus first-time buyers: about 6,100 SBA change-of-ownership loans a year (3,043 in the first half of FY2026 × 2, [Coleman Report](https://colemanreport.com/main-street-monday-one-in-four-sba-7a-lending-dollars-finances-business-acquisitions/)), of which an estimated 1,200-1,800 are large enough to pay.
11. **Strongest argument against.** A crowded, capital-heavy race. Each platform eventually moves to one ERP and hires a controller. Sponsors may want a US CPA firm's name on the numbers, and PE firms push vendors toward SOC 2 ([Barnes Dennig](https://www.barnesdennig.com/soc-reporting-for-private-equity-companies/)).

### Card 8. Vendor bank-change fraud and AI-forged invoices (Step 1 #46)

**Weaker as a stand-alone service. Losses are real and growing, but software comes with $1 million guarantees, banks now must monitor, and the check has to happen live in US hours.**

1. **The task.** An AP clerk gets a request to change a supplier's bank account. They freeze payments, call the supplier on the number already on file (never the one in the email), run an account-ownership check, get a second approver, log the evidence, and release or block before the next payment run. The change can come through the supplier's own portal: the City of Baltimore paid $1,524,621.04 to a fraudster who changed bank details in a vendor's Workday account ([Security Affairs](https://securityaffairs.com/181772/cyber-crime/fraudster-stole-over-1-5-million-from-city-of-baltimore.html)).
2. **Who feels it, who pays.** AP and vendor-master specialists feel it daily. The controller or CFO signs, usually only after a loss.
3. **How often, how many hours.** Daily to weekly. Estimate for a mid-market firm: 5-20 bank changes a week at 15-30 minutes, plus 5-14 suspicious invoices at 10-20 minutes, gives 2-15 hours a week (9-65 hours a month).
4. **What it costs a month.** Estimate: staff time $290-$2,600 a month. Expected loss: the average BEC complaint is about $123K ($3.046B ÷ 24,768). At an assumed 5-25% yearly chance of one, that is $0.5K-$2.6K a month. Total about $0.8K-$5.2K a month, with a fat tail of single losses above $1.5M.
5. **How it is handled today.** Manual callbacks and dual approval. BILL emails the vendor to confirm a change, but confirmation is not required before payment ([BILL help](https://vendorpayhelp.bill.com/hc/en-us/articles/360056648731)). Bank positive pay. Larger firms use Trustpair or Eftsure, both quote-priced.
6. **Proof.** FBI IC3 2025: BEC losses of $3.046B from 24,768 complaints, and a first-ever AI section with about $893M in losses ([iTnews](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/ai-assisted-fraud-makes-big-debut-in-fbis-cybercrime-stats-624819)). AFP 2026: over 75% of US firms faced attempted or actual payments fraud in 2025, and only 17% use AI against it ([AFP](https://www.financialprofessionals.org/about/learn-more/press-releases/Details/over-75-percent-of-us-firms-experienced-payments-fraud-in-2025-while-ai-adoption-for-fraud-mitigation-lags)). A practitioner in AFP coverage: "One of the things that used to tip you off was a grammatical error or a spelling error" ([AFP](https://www.financialprofessionals.org/training-resources/resources/articles/Details/from-bec-to-deepfakes-payments-fraud-is-only-getting-more-sophisticated)). An IOFM expert: "There is no industry standard for holding payments…" ([IOFM](https://www.iofm.com/ask-the-expert/vendor-banking-change-safeguards-and-payment-hold-guidelines)). Australian payment-redirection losses rose 9.3% to A$166.8M in 2025 ([Eftsure](https://www.eftsure.com/blog/industry-news/$167m-lost-in-2025-why-bec-scams-keep-costing-australians/), vendor, citing ACCC data).
7. **Who else sells a fix.** Trustpair (500+ enterprise customers, up to $1M liability cover, [ERP Research](https://erpresearch.com/erp-add-ons/fraud-risk/trustpair)); Eftsure ($1M guarantee, Nacha Preferred Partner, [Eftsure pricing](https://eftsure.com/pricing)); Early Warning, Plaid, Paymode, AppZen, Medius, Coupa with Trustpair. Software: moderate to high. Done-for-you service: thin.
8. **Next 24 months.** Attacks grow, but the human share of defence shrinks. New Nacha rules from 2026 make banks monitor for payments "authorized under false pretenses" ([Nacha](https://www.nacha.org/rules/risk-management-topics-fraud-monitoring-phase-2)). Voice cloning weakens the callback itself. In the UK, invoice and mandate fraud fell to multi-year lows once banks had to act ([Payment Expert](https://paymentexpert.com/2026/06/15/uk-finance-fraud-report-2026/)).
9. **How to reach buyers.** Commercial crime-insurance brokers (policies demand callback controls), AFP, IOFM, the Nacha partner programme, Eftsure or Trustpair partner deals, and Coupa, BILL and NetSuite ecosystems.
10. **How many US companies.** About 107,700 US firms with 100+ employees (2021 Census data via an [Apollo chart](https://apolloacademy.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/070724-Chart.pdf)). Estimate: about 82,000 face attempted or actual fraud each year (× 76%), likely an overstatement.
11. **Strongest argument against.** Courts tend to put the loss on whoever was "best positioned" to stop it ([Arnall Golden Gregory](https://www.agg.com/news-insights/publications/business-email-compromise-fraud-should-the-party-best-positioned-to-avoid-the-fraud-bear-the-loss/)), which would be the verifier. Software carries guarantees a small firm cannot match.

### Card 9. AI-faked damage photos and refund claims (Step 1 #47)

**Weaker after the deep dive. The fraud is real and rising, but the loss per brand is small, cheap video-capture apps stop much of it, and the customer-service buyer has no fraud budget.**

1. **The task.** A customer-service agent opens each "arrived damaged" claim. They check the photos or video, the order, the customer's history, and whether images are reused or AI-made. Then they approve, deny, or ask for live video or a return.
2. **Who feels it, who pays.** CX agents and the returns manager feel it. The head of CX, COO or founder signs. At Boll & Branch, the CEO spotted a faked $489 claim himself ([eCommerce Fastlane](https://ecommercefastlane.com/ai-generated-fakes-shopify-merchants-2026/)).
3. **How often, how many hours.** Daily. Estimate for a $20M-GMV brand: 200,000 orders at an assumed $100 average; 1-3% raise photo claims, so 40-115 a week; at 2-5 minutes each, 1.5-10 hours a week.
4. **What it costs a month.** Estimate: if 5-10% of those claims are AI-faked at about $100 each, the loss is $0.8K-$5K a month. Industry-wide, Appriss puts 2025 returns fraud and abuse at about $100B on $706B of returns ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260219293604/en/Appriss-Retail-Benchmark-Report-Uncovers-%24796B-in-Total-Retail-Loss), vendor claim). NRF and Happy Returns figures imply about $76B (estimate: 9% × $849.9B). The two use different definitions.
5. **How it is handled today.** Agents eyeball photos. Returns apps collect photos and apply rules (AfterShip from $23 a month, Loop about $155). Fraud vendors charge per transaction (Riskified about 0.4%). DamageProof forces live, timestamped video for $0, $29 or $99 a month ([Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/damageproof-io)). AI-image detection APIs cost $0.0005-$0.04 per image.
6. **Proof.** Ravelin says 67% of merchants received AI-generated fake refund evidence (June 2026, vendor survey, [The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/fraud-and-fincrime/thought-leader-insights/ravelin-report-key-takeaways-global-fraud-trends-2026)). Modern Retail reported Boll & Branch and Bogg Bag cases on 2 March 2026 ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/technology/from-boll-branch-to-bogg-brands-are-battling-a-surge-of-ai-driven-return-fraud/)). Dedicated returns-fraud jobs exist only at very large retailers, such as Abercrombie & Fitch (posted 3 May 2026, [Swooped](https://swooped.co/job-postings/associate-analyst-returns-af-co-columbus-aampf-management-co-00476)).
7. **Who else sells a fix.** Forter, Riskified and Signifyd (returns-abuse modules); Loop, AfterShip and Redo; DamageProof; shipping-protection apps with claim scoring (Corso, Route, Protega); generic image-forensics APIs. Software: medium crowding. Done-for-you review: empty, which may mean buyers will not pay for it.
8. **Next 24 months.** Attacks grow. But fakes painted onto real photos defeat whole-image detectors, so defence moves to capture controls (live video, media-reuse checks), which are cheap software, not human review.
9. **How to reach buyers.** Shopify App Store; returns and shipping-protection partner ecosystems; helpdesk marketplaces; eCommerce Fastlane, Practical Ecommerce; the British Retail Consortium in the UK.
10. **How many US companies.** 1,070,098 live US Shopify stores in February 2026 ([LinkMyBooks citing StoreLeads](https://linkmybooks.com/blog/how-many-sellers-are-on-shopify)). Estimate: 5,000-20,000 above $5M GMV (low confidence).
11. **Strongest argument against.** Small loss per brand, a $29 app that solves much of it, and a two-sided risk: wrongly denying real customers costs customers.

### Card 10. Australia AML/CTF Tranche 2 client due diligence (Step 1 #31)

**Stronger after the deep dive. The law is live, about half the market had not even enrolled by the deadline, the regulator is sending letters, and the law allows a firm to rely on an outside team under a written agreement.**

1. **The task.** For each new client on a covered service (selling a house, setting up a company or trust, holding client money), staff collect ID for every person. For companies and trusts they trace who really owns or controls them. They screen names against sanctions and politically exposed person lists, rate the risk, ask where the money comes from for higher-risk clients, file the evidence, keep monitoring, and lodge reports to AUSTRAC when needed. Real estate agents must verify both buyers and sellers ([Didit](https://didit.me/blog/austrac-tranche-2-real-estate-agents-guide/), vendor guide).
2. **Who feels it, who pays.** The firm's AML/CTF compliance officer, who must be a person inside the firm. In small practices that is a partner, principal or practice manager "already carrying a full workload" ([CCP Accountants](https://www.ccp.com.au/news/tranche-2-aml-ctf-obligations-accountants/)). The same person signs.
3. **How often, how many hours.** Every new covered client. Estimates: a real estate agency with 10 sales a month has 20 files at about 28 minutes, plus monitoring, so about 12 hours a month. A small accounting practice with 8 new covered clients a month needs about 9-10 hours. A conveyancer with 30 matters needs about 12.5 hours plus entity matters.
4. **What it costs a month.** Estimate for a small agency: A$760-A$2,600 a month (US$490-$1,690 at 0.65) in staff time plus ID-check fees. The government's own impact statement estimates up to A$28,650 upfront and A$23,250 a year ongoing for an average small business, about A$1,940 a month ([Scantek](https://scantek.com/how-much-will-aml-ctf-tranche-2-compliance-cost-your-business/), quoting the statement).
5. **How it is handled today.** Free law-society templates ([Law Society NSW](https://www.lawsociety.com.au/new-guidance-help-small-practice-amlctf-compliance)); program software from A$490 a year; consultant set-ups of A$4,500-A$11,000 (vendor page, unverified); per-check ID tools from US$0.15 ([Didit](https://didit.me/blog/austrac-tranche-2-aml-ctf-customer-due-diligence/)); fully outsourced managed services such as AML Partners, which claims 300-400 clients ([AAP](https://www.aap.com.au/aapreleases/globenewswire9804986/), vendor claim).
6. **Proof.** Obligations began 1 July 2026. By the 29 July enrolment deadline, only about 50% of accounting practices, 40% of real estate agencies and 12-14% of law firms had enrolled, and AUSTRAC began sending section 167 notices on 28 August 2026 ([Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/08/austrac-takes-compliance-action-in-relation-to-tra)). In an Access Group survey of 1,700+ accountants, only 6% felt on top of the rules and 32% had not started ([Accounting Times](https://www.accountingtimes.com.au/profession/access-group-warns-of-guidance-gap-ahead-of-tranche-2-aml-ctf), vendor-run). Access Group's David Boyar said small practices "with no dedicated compliance resource face acute operational pressure" (paraphrase in the same coverage).
7. **Who else sells a fix.** First AML (REIA's preferred AML partner; six of the top ten law firms, vendor claim, [AAP](https://www.aap.com.au/aapreleases/cision20250702ae22703)); InfoTrack with LEAP; Forms Live integrations (easyAML, AMLHUB, APLYiD); Didit, Persona, NameScan and Scantek; AML Partners; Kit Legal; consultants. ### Bet 1: The checked monthly close for small roll-ups and first-time owners

**The offer in one line: "Your group close, mapped, checked and commented by business day 10, signed off by a named reviewer."**

**Why this one.** Buyers already pay a lot. A multi-entity controller costs $120K-$175K base, and a fractional CFO $6K-$25K a month (Card 7). **How it makes money (estimate, not tested with buyers).** About $1,500 a month base, plus $200-$450 per entity a month, plus $1,500-$4,000 one-time per new add-on (pricing shape from the deep-dive notes). ## Conclusion

**The value in AI operations has moved from doing the keystrokes to owning the deadline and signing the result.** In 2025 and 2026, incumbents shipped AI for the very workarounds this scan found: Applied Recon for commission matching, WellSky and Brightree for fax intake, athenahealth for denials, and CargoWise for customs invoices. A services firm that sells keystrokes will be undercut by a feature. One that sells a checked, accountable outcome by a fixed date still gets paid, because a sponsor, a regulator or a payer holds someone responsible.

For Wanile, that turns the Lahore time zone from a handicap into an asset, but only for overnight and month-end work. It also makes compliance posture part of the product. SOC 2, written reliance agreements, US-hosted environments and a named reviewer will do more to win the first US client than a better agent will. Pick the deadline-driven work, build that trust layer early, and let the 14-day tests decide which of the three bets gets your next six months.
