The big idea

Typing is cheap now. Owning the result is not.

Think of the work as a ladder. The higher you go, the more people pay.

Top step · still pays well
Own the result

The job is done by a deadline, checked, and a named person signs it.

Example: a US group controller costs $120K to $175K a year. Companies pay that because someone must own the numbers.
▲
Middle step · paid if tied to a result
Check the work

Catch errors, chase missing papers, make judgement calls a tool cannot make.

▲
Bottom step · now very cheap
Type it in

Read a document and enter it into software.

Now about $1 to $2 per order, about $2.75 per faxed referral, and AI phone answering from $29 a month.

In 2025 and 2026, the big software companies added AI for exactly these typing jobs. A service that only types will lose to a free feature. A service that owns a checked result by a fixed date still gets paid.

Why the order changed

Where your team sits matters by law too

US customs

Broker records must stay inside the US, and a licensed US broker must supervise. This pushed customs down to 7th.

US health data

US health privacy law (HIPAA) allows offshore work with a signed contract. But Texas Medicaid bars patient data leaving the US, Tennessee needs written consent, and some insurers ask for offshore forms.

Defense parts

Defense drawings cannot be shown to foreign persons (ITAR). This hurt the price-quote idea for machine shops.

Australia

Good news. Firms may rely on an outside team under a written agreement. The firm stays responsible, which is exactly a human-in-the-loop service.

US data rule

Good news. The US rule on bulk personal data covers only six countries, and Pakistan is not one of them.

That is why price quotes (42) fell to 4th place, even with the biggest dollar prize. Winning there means answering within an hour in US daytime, which is a night shift in Lahore.

Bet 1 · US

Monthly books for groups of small companies

46of 70
What you sell, in one line"Your group's month-end report, cleaned, checked and explained by day 10, signed by a named reviewer."

What is going on

Investment firms (private equity) buy one company. Then they keep buying small ones in the same trade: heating and cooling, vet clinics, dental, IT, accounting. 74.9% of US buyouts so far in 2026 were these add-ons.

Each new company comes with its own messy books. Every month, someone must join them all into one clean report for the owners. Tools like Fathom and Reach can add up the numbers, but only after a person has cleaned and matched every company's books first. That cleaning is the pain.

How the service works

1
10 to 30 small companiesEach has its own QuickBooks or Xero books.
2
Your team cleans and matchesAI maps every account to one group list and flags odd numbers. Accountants fix the errors.
3
One group report, by day 10With short comments on what changed and why.
4
A named reviewer signsThe client approves the final numbers.

Who pays, and what they pay today

The buyer is the group's CFO or controller, or the CEO in small groups. For people who just bought one business (first-time owners), it is the owner.

$120K to $175Ka year for a group controller (2026 job posts)
$6K to $25Ka month for a part-time CFO (seller's figure)
$1.5K to $4K+a month for outsourced multi-company bookkeeping (seller's figure)
What you could charge (estimate, not tested yet)
Monthly base fee$1,500
Per company, per month$200 to $450
One-time, per new company added$1,500 to $4,000
First-time owner (one company)$1,200 to $2,500 / mo
A 14-company group pays about $4,300 to $7,800 a month10 such clients would be about $60K a month. That is still cheaper for them than hiring a controller (about $19K a month all-in).

Why it fits Wanile

  • Monthly deadline, not live work. Your day shift finishes while the US sleeps.
  • You have built this before. It is the same kind of work as your bookkeeping agent for the Danish firm.
  • The model is accepted. Offshore finance teams already serve these investment firms.

How to win clients without cold selling

  • Free 72-hour check: rebuild last month's group report from their numbers and show what was off.
  • Partner accountants listed in the Fathom, Reach and Intuit ProAdvisor directories. They sell tools, not the clean-up.
  • ACG events (58 to 61 chapters) and the investors' operating partners, for referrals.
  • SBA lenders and the SearchFunder community, for first-time owners.
Watch out for
  • It is getting crowded. One AI accounting group (Crete) plans to spend $500M+ buying accounting firms.
  • Each group may later hire its own controller or move to one system.
  • Buyers may want a SOC 2 security report and a US CPA name. Plan SOC 2 within 12 to 18 months, or partner with a small US CPA firm.
Halal note (a flag, not a ruling)

Most of these groups carry interest-bearing loans. Doing the company's own books is different from serving the lender. Leave out lender covenant certificates, and ask your scholar.

Bet 2 · Australia

Client checks for Australian law, accounting and real estate firms

45of 70
What you sell, in one line"We prepare every client check, find who really owns the company or trust, re-check monthly and keep the records. Your compliance officer approves."

What is going on

Australia's money-laundering law (AML/CTF) used to cover mostly banks. Since 1 July 2026, it also covers lawyers, accountants, conveyancers and real estate agents. They must now check who every new client really is, and where the money comes from. The regulator, AUSTRAC, expects the businesses it watches to grow from about 19,000 to close to 100,000.

About 50%of accounting practices had signed up by the 29 July deadline. Real estate: 40%. Law firms: 12% to 14%.
28 Aug 2026AUSTRAC started sending formal notices to firms that had not signed up.
Only 6%of accountants surveyed felt on top of the rules (survey run by a software seller).
A$23,250a year: the government's own estimate of ongoing cost for an average small business.

How the service works

1
A new client walks inA house sale, a new company or trust, or client money held in trust.
2
The firm sends you the fileUnder a written agreement, which the law allows.
3
Your desk does the checkingID check, find the real owners, screen sanction lists, draft the risk rating.
4
Their compliance officer approvesThe law says this person must sit inside the firm.
5
You keep it currentStore the records and re-screen clients every month.

Why it fits Wanile

  • Best clock match on the list. Your workday covers Sydney's afternoon and evening. Files sent after lunch are ready by morning.
  • The law allows it. A firm may rely on an outside team under a written agreement.
  • You have proof. Your compliance AI for a US bank and your legal AI are direct examples.

How to win clients without cold selling

Note: this does not give you the first US client logo you want. It gives you revenue and a strong compliance case study to show US buyers.

Watch out for
  • Small price per firm.
  • AUSTRAC promised not to chase small firms over small slips in year one.
  • Rivals sit inside tools firms already use: First AML is the real estate body's partner, and InfoTrack works with LEAP.
  • Cheap ID-check tools (from US$0.15 a check) and free law society templates.
  • Some firms will simply stop offering the covered services.
Halal note (a flag, not a ruling)

Do not take lenders, casinos or bookmakers as clients. Decide up front about precious-metal dealers. Most property sales use interest-based mortgages, which is worth asking your scholar about.

Bet 3 · US

Getting back money insurers did not pay doctors

44of 70
What you sell, in one line"We check 90 days of paid claims against your insurance contracts and work the old denials nobody touched. You pay a share of what comes back."

What is going on

Insurers refuse some claims (a denial). On others they pay less than the contract says (an underpayment). Underpaid claims look "paid", so nobody checks them.

48%of practice leaders said denials are their biggest money leak (MGMA poll, January 2026).
41%of providers have denial rates of 10% or more (Experian, 2025).
$9.6K to $22Ka month a 5-doctor practice may leave on the table (estimate).

How the service works

1
A billing company or practice shares data90 days of paid claims, plus their insurance contracts.
2
AI compares paid vs. promisedIt finds short payments and sorts the old denials.
3
Your team writes the appealsFix and resubmit. This part does not need live calls.
4
Money comes backYou keep a share of what is recovered.
How it makes money (estimate)
Direct to a practice20% to 25% of money recovered
Through a billing companyabout 10% to 15% (a guess)
Up to $2,400 to $5,500 a month per practiceIf you recover everything, at 25%. One billing company with 30 clients multiplies this. Today practices already pay billing firms 4% to 10% of collections, and denial firms 25%.

Why it fits Wanile

  • The work can wait for your day. Checking contracts and writing appeals do not need live calls.
  • It sells through partners. The billing companies' association (HBMA) has nearly 500 member firms.
  • The weak spot: you have no healthcare billing history yet. This is your weakest fit of the three.
Watch out for
  • Very crowded. athenahealth and WebPT launched AI denial tools in mid-2026. Commure/Athelas sells full billing to small practices.
  • Sign a business associate agreement (the HIPAA contract). Keep patient data on a US-hosted system with no local copies.
  • Avoid Texas and Tennessee Medicaid data unless consent is in place. Be ready for insurers' offshore forms.
  • No bots on insurer portals. Availity, a main portal, bans them.
Halal note (a flag, not a ruling)

The money comes from conventional insurers, and a share-of-recovery fee ties your income to insurer money. Please ask your scholar before you start.

Things you did not ask about

3 surprises from the research

1
Your exact business model already has investor money behind it.

Glimpse, a funded startup, is hiring people in the Philippines to be "the expert human-in-the-loop" behind its AI. Y Combinator's 2026 wish list asks for AI-native agencies that sell finished work, not tools.

So "AI plus humans" alone will not set you apart. Your edge must come from the niche you pick, the partners who send you work, and how trusted your security and compliance are.

2
The famous new laws mostly died. The money is in boring rules with teeth.

Courts cancelled the FinCEN real estate rule and the FTC "click to cancel" rule. The food tracing rule slipped to 2028.

The weekly work sits in rules where someone is already getting sued or warned. Washington's pay-transparency law has drawn 300+ class actions since June 2024. AUSTRAC is already mailing notices.

3
Where your team sits matters by law, not just by time zone.

US customs records must stay in the US. Texas Medicaid bars patient data leaving the US. Defense drawings cannot be shown to foreign persons.

But some doors are open: the US bulk data rule does not include Pakistan, and Australia openly allows outside client-check providers.

One more: in February 2026 the US Supreme Court struck down a big set of tariffs. By mid-September, about $134.7B of refunds had been accepted. That created real paid work for customs firms, but it fades by late 2027.
