# Track 6: Google search demand

## 1. Track name and scope
Track 6. Google search demand and paid search for vibe-code rescue terms.

What I could NOT do: open Google Trends (WebFetch to trends.google.com failed with "getaddrinfo ENOTFOUND trends.google.com"), Google autocomplete, or the Ads Transparency Center. No raw Google data was seen by me at any point.

What I did: 12 WebSearch queries that returned results (all standard mode, cache: `cache/06-google-searches.md`). Four more were refused because the shared WebSearch budget ran out (limit 200 calls per turn, shared by all agents). I did not work around it. Those four were: an Exploding Topics search, a "vibe coding searches are up" Google Trends search, a Semrush/Ahrefs lovable.dev search, and the adstransparency.google.com paid-ads search. So item (d) paid search ads was NOT tested at all, and item (c) People also ask was not found. About 25 planned queries were never run (see section 2, finding 13).

Time spent: well under the 60 minutes. Only snippets and tool-written summaries were read. No page was opened. All rankings are a PROXY: they show what this web search tool returned (US-only, engine unknown, not Google), not the real Google results page. Do not treat them as Google rankings.

## 6. What this means for the offer
- Use buyer words that agencies already rank for: "fix", "cleanup", "audit", "hire a Lovable/Replit/Bolt developer". Do not use "rescue" alone; "ai app rescue" returns emergency apps.
- Organic search is crowded on "cleanup" and "hire X developer" (7 of 9 results for "vibe code cleanup service" are agency pages; most results for "lovable developer for hire" are agencies too). Expect SEO to be slow and paid search or long-tail pages to be the entry point. Nothing here gives a cost per click.
- Price anchors visible on ranking pages: $19 to $39 scanners at the bottom, Fiverr fixes in the hundreds, one seller at $2,500 for a week, about $3K for a one-week contractor engagement, $5K to $15K per project from an agency guide. Our $2,000 per week sits inside the visible range, but only one seller-published page here shows a buyer-paid price at that level, and no buyer evidence supports it.

## 7. What this means for the GO gates
- Gate 1 (buyers paying $1,500+): unclear. Seller pages quote $2,500/week and about $3K; no buyer payment seen in this track.
- Gate 2 (channel under 30% of project value): unclear. No keyword volume, CPC or paid-ad evidence for rescue terms was obtained. Paid search was not tested.
- Gate 4 (position above the low-cost floor): unclear, leaning contradicts. Search results show a $19 to $39 scanner floor, many Fiverr fixes, and large outsourcing agencies with dedicated cleanup pages; Lovable also runs its own Expert directory.
- Gate 5 (platform improvements): unclear. The head term "vibe coding" has plateaued since early 2026 (weak, second-hand), which does not show a shrinking rescue demand either way.
