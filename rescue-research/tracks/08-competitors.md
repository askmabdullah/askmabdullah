## 6. What this means for the offer
- A transparent weekly fixed price around $2,000 to $2,500 fits a gap: Forasoft's $2,500 start is the only close match; most agencies hide prices and push a free audit first. Slopfix at $10,000 per week shows a premium buyer exists but it sells deletion for larger codebases, not finishing small apps.
- Free audits are the norm (at least 6 sellers). A paid audit credited to the sprint (Rocking Tech model, Valletta from $199, Relevant from $900) is the way to filter tyre-kickers while matching what buyers already see. Do not give a free multi-day audit.
- The only guarantee seen is Slopfix's two-week warranty. A clear warranty plus named case studies would stand out, since none of the snippets showed either.

## 7. What this means for the GO gates
- Gate 1 (buyers paying $1,500+): unclear. Sellers publish $900, $2,500, $10,000 and $50-150k prices, but no buyer payment is shown. Seller prices are not proof of sales.
- Gate 2 (channel under 30% of project value): unclear. No ad evidence found; Ad Library not reachable here.
- Gate 3 (repeatable work): unclear. Seller pages list the same fix themes (auth, security, tests, CI, Supabase); that hints at repeatability but is marketing.
- Gate 4 (position above the low-cost floor): supports, weakly. Floor is $10 to $50 gigs; agency audits start at $199 to $900; weekly sprints start near $2,500. A $2,000 weekly sprint sits above the floor and at the low end of agencies, so the position exists but is not unique.
- Gate 5 (platform improvements): not touched by this track. Forasoft sells fixes for cases where Lovable's built-in "Try to Fix" fails, which shows platform fix tools are not enough today.

Market verdict: crowded for generic "vibe coding cleanup" (12 or more agencies with dedicated pages, mostly Eastern/Central European and US mid-size firms, SEO-driven, free-audit funnels), open at a transparent small-ticket weekly price with a warranty. Confidence: low to moderate, because only 9 searches ran and all prices are snippet text.
