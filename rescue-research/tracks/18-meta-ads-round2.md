## 6. What this means for the offer
- Plan the first 30 days as a paid test on our own numbers, not as a copy of a proven playbook. No competitor is shown to have run Meta ads for this, so there is no benchmark funnel to copy and no sign of crowded ad auctions either.
- Free audits are the visible front door among sellers (MetaCTO 48 hours, McKelvey 20 minutes, ULAM, Chudovo). Meta can feed that. A paid audit credited to the sprint is a way to filter out low-intent leads and to avoid competing on free.
- The only $15K+ rescue price seen is a seller's own page (McKelvey, $15K-$100K, 4-8 weeks). Our $2,000 per week price sits under that and above the $30-$500 floor. It is a vendor claim, so do not treat it as proof buyers pay it.

## 7. What this means for the GO gates
- Gate 1 (buyers paying $1,500+): unclear. McKelvey's page lists $15K-$100K, but it is a seller price, not a buyer payment. No buyer evidence in this track.
- Gate 2 (cost per paying client under about $600 on a $2,000 project): unclear leaning against. Raw Meta CPL is about $27 all-industry but B2B vendor figures are $50-$380 per lead, software development is $510-$680 per lead and $1,680-$3,080 per qualified lead in vendor tables. Using a booked-call cost of $80-$150, show rate 75-85% and close rate 20-30% gives roughly $400 to $1,000 per client (my arithmetic, see below). That is an assumption stack from non-software, vendor sources. It could pass or fail. Meta has not been shown to work for this niche.
- Gate 4 (position above floor): unclear. Seller prices cluster between $199 audits and $15K+ projects; no ad data.
- Gates 3 and 5: not touched.

Arithmetic note for gate 2 (my calculation, not a sourced figure): cost per client = cost per booked call / (show rate x close rate). With $80 per call, 80% show, 25% close: $80 / 0.20 = $400. With $150 per call, 75% show, 20% close: $150 / 0.15 = $1,000. Range about $400 to $1,000. Those inputs are vendor or other-niche figures. The $600 gate sits inside this range.
