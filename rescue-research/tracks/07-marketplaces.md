## 6. What this means for the offer
- A $2,000 rescue would sit 20x above the $20 median Fiverr starting price and 2x above the highest fix/finish job seen on Upwork ($1,000). The offer must not compete on price with these gigs. It needs a different buyer.
- The marketplaces show the same fix list again and again: Supabase RLS and auth, Stripe, deploy, white screens, exposed keys. That list can be the base of a standard checklist.
- Marketplaces look like a poor first channel for $2,000 jobs. They could still work as a cheap lead source for a low-priced paid audit, but this track has no evidence for that.

## 7. What this means for the GO gates
- Gate 1 (buyers paying $1,500+): contradicts, weakly. Top fix/finish price seen is $1,000 (Upwork, 2026-04-03). Partial search only (8 searches). Not proof of absence.
- Gate 3 (repeatable work): supports, weakly. Sellers list the same failure types across dozens of gigs (vendor marketing).
- Gate 4 (position above the low-cost floor): unclear. The floor is $5 to $20 on Fiverr, which is a very large gap to $2,000. Whether buyers see the difference was not tested here.
- Gate 2 (channel cost) and gate 5 (platform improvement): not touched.
