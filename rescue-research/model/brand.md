## 1. The brand decision

### 1.1 Three options

- A. Wanile brand. Rescue sits on wanile's own site and name.
- B. Endorsed sub-brand. Own name, own domain, own page. Wanile named openly as the company behind it.
- C. Fully separate brand. Wanile not named.

### 1.2 Reasons FOR keeping it on the Wanile brand (A)

1. Trust assets live under the Wanile name.
   - Wanile has a paid code audit plus fix contract for an AI SaaS, a rebuilt legal AI platform, and clients in the UK, US, Europe, Australia and the Middle East [brief s1].
   - Buyers say proof beats location: references, past-client reviews, live case studies [t15 #4, #7][t20 #18].
2. A new brand starts at zero reviews.
   - Small Lahore agencies on Clutch show 16 to 61 reviews (CodeNinja 53, Code District 35, Chakor 61, Build Me App 16) [t20 #20].
   - The best Fiverr rescue sellers show 62 to 86 reviews [t21 #10].
   - Zero reviews is a visible gap next to those.
3. Capacity signal. 20 engineers is a real selling point against solo sellers and 3-person shops (Slopfix has 3 engineers) [t08].
4. One brand is cheaper. One site, one Clutch page, one Upwork history, one LinkedIn page.
5. Rescue clients are a feeder. Rescue to maintenance to a dedicated engineer is the planned path [brief s2]. Market anchors for a dedicated Pakistan-based developer are $3,000 to $8,000 a month (vendor pages, weak) [t14][t20 #15].

### 1.3 Reasons AGAINST the Wanile brand (for B or C)

1. Buyer segment mismatch.
   - Wanile's main brand is moving to AI operations and AI-native engineering for SMEs [brief].
   - Rescue buyers are non-technical builders with small budgets, many price-shopping on Upwork and Fiverr [t17][t21].
   - One site speaking to both confuses both. The SME ops buyer sees "fix your Lovable app" and reads "junior shop".
2. Price point sits next to the floor.
   - Fiverr fix gigs: min $5, median $25, mean $40.8, max $250 across 50 gigs [t21 #9].
   - Upwork productized tiers: $49 to $750 [t21 #3].
   - Pakistani sellers at $17/hr and $40 to $100 gigs [t21 #5].
   - A $2,000 sprint on the main brand pulls that comparison onto Wanile's name.
3. Platform program caps.
   - Lovable's Expert track is for individuals and agencies under 5 people [t09 #1, #2][t06 #10]. Wanile at 20 engineers is above the cap.
   - The Solution Partner track is built for 5+ people selling Lovable Enterprise and Business licences. It is a poor fit for rescue and sends no rescue leads [t09 s6].
   - Caveat: Lovable's own pages conflict. One says the Experts program "is no longer active" and was replaced by the Solution Partner Program [t20 #7]. Lead volume to experts: none found [t09 s6].
4. Reputation and security risk.
   - We will touch apps with live user data. A breach, dispute or chargeback is tied to whatever name is on the contract.
   - Fixed-price rescue has scope-creep and refund risk, and no dispute stories could be searched [t19 #6].
   - A separate name limits public damage to the main brand. It does NOT limit legal liability if the same entity signs (see 1.6).
5. Reversibility.
   - A sub-brand folds into Wanile with a redirect in a day.
   - A rescue offer that goes wrong on the Wanile name is hard to detach.
6. SEO. This argument is weak both ways.
   - Rescue terms are held by agency pages: 7 of 9 results for "vibe code cleanup service" are agency pages with a dedicated cleanup page [t06 #11].
   - "Fix my lovable app" is 5 of 9 Fiverr gigs [t06 #7].
   - One seller says SEO "has stopped being a useful avenue for a project pipeline quite a while back" (HN, seller, anecdote) [t18].
   - Wanile's main domain authority would help a rescue page a little. A focused domain helps with exact search words. No data says which wins. Do not pick the brand on SEO.

### 1.4 Reasons AGAINST full separation (C)

1. Hiding Wanile removes the best proof (named case studies, 20 engineers, record).
2. It clashes with the "show Lahore" stance in section 3. A hidden parent is a worse story than an open one.
3. Platform and Clutch profiles show the company and location anyway (section 3).
4. A Lovable Expert application with a hidden 20-person parent could breach program terms. Terms can change at any time [t09 #2]. Disclose the link.

### 1.5 The pick

**Pick B: an endorsed sub-brand.**

Why, in short lines:
- It keeps the trust assets of Wanile (A's best reason).
- It keeps the rescue buyer away from the upmarket story (the segment, price and floor reasons).
- It can apply to the Lovable Expert track as a small named team, if Lovable allows it (see 1.7).
- It is cheap to merge back and cheap to cut off. That matters because Gate 1 and Gate 2 are still unclear.
- It matches what the platform-program track already suggested: "use a small separate brand for the under-5-person Expert track" [t09 s7].

Confidence: moderate-low. The call rests on judgment, not on a conversion test.

### 1.6 Practical setup

**Name and domain**
- New name (candidates in section 6). New .com, or .dev if the .com is taken.
- Do not put "Lovable", "Bolt", "Replit" or "Base44" in the brand name or domain. Platform trademark risk. Lawyer check. (My note, not from a track.)
- Use words buyers and sellers actually search: "fix", "finish", "audit", "cleanup", "hire ... developer". "Rescue" alone is a weak keyword, because "ai app rescue" returns animal and emergency rescue apps [t06 #14].
- Business email on the new domain (hello@, audit@). Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC.

**Legal entity**
- Start with Wanile Technologies as the contracting party. The brand is a trading name. Invoices in USD, as Wanile already does [brief s1].
- Reason: Wanile has the track record, the bank and the IP chain. A fresh entity adds cost and no history.
- No track found any evidence that a Western-registered entity, a US/UK phone number, or Stripe invoicing changes buyer trust [t15 "NOT FOUND"]. Big Pakistani firms do lead with US addresses (Arbisoft: Plano, Texas first; 10Pearls: Virginia; Tkxel: Reston, Virginia) [t15 #8]. That is what sellers do, not proof it converts.
- Do NOT use a US or UK address Wanile does not really have. Use a real address only.
- Liability: if Wanile signs, Wanile carries the liability. A ring-fenced entity is a real option. This is for model/risk.md and a lawyer. Revisit after the first 10 paid sprints or on the first serious dispute.
- Contract text must say "[Brand], a trading name of Wanile Technologies [legal suffix]". Check trading-name rules with a lawyer.

**Who fronts it**
- One named human as the public face. Suggested: the Wanile founder (Muhammad), if he agrees.
- One named senior engineer as delivery lead, with photo and short bio.
- Reason: solo and small sellers sell on a personal name (Hamid Siddiqi on Fiverr, 15 to 20 regular clients; Justin McKelvey; Upwork profiles) [t19 #11][t21 #4]. No track tested whether a founder profile builds trust [t15 "NOT FOUND"]. So this is a test, not a fact.
- Keep the face count honest and small. If a Lovable Expert application is made, the named team must match the real team (see 1.7).

**How it links to Wanile**
- Footer on every page: "A service of Wanile Technologies, Lahore, Pakistan" with a link.
- About page names Wanile, shows its past projects (with permission), and states 20 engineers.
- Contract, invoices, privacy page and Stripe descriptor show Wanile.
- Wanile's own site: a quiet link only (footer or "Studio" page). Not in the main nav. Upmarket SME visitors do not need it.
- Reviews: pool them on Wanile's Clutch page and Upwork agency history. Ask each rescue client to name the project type in the review. This avoids starting at zero and keeps one reputation. Upwork "specialized profile" for rescue inside the Wanile agency is the way to keep job history (my note; check the feature).
- Retention path shown on the page: "When you outgrow the sprint, Wanile's engineers can take over" (the dedicated-engineer step).

### 1.7 The Lovable Expert track (a side channel, not the reason for the brand)

- Track facts: free application, answer in about 5 working days, paid Lovable plan needed (Pro is enough), no exam. Assessed on published projects, traffic and build quality. Certified Lovable Expert badge. 10% commission on Business plans referred, 12 months [t09 #2].
- Value is unproven: no lead volume, no reviews, no counts of experts found [t09 s6].
- The top-hired expert in a cached directory is also the cheapest (Weekend Inc, 100 hires, $50/hr; Neo Carbone 28 hires $130/hr) [t20 #8]. Weak, possibly stale.
- If we apply: the brand's profile lists the real people (4 or fewer) who work on Lovable projects. Say in the application that the brand is run by Wanile Technologies. Ask Lovable in writing if that is allowed.
- Do not fake a small team to pass the cap. If Lovable says no, drop the track. The brand decision does not depend on it.
- Build Lovable projects first. Programs reward shipped projects with traffic [t09 s7].

Plain reading:
- $2,000 is the median of the studio tier, not above it. At least 8 small vendors already list there [t21 #7, #8][sum gate 4].
- Against a $2,500 EU studio, $2,000 is only 20% cheaper. Buyers already expect a discount for Pakistan [t15 #3]. A 20% gap will not cover the trust gap. Proof has to do it.
- Most agencies hide prices. Of 29 agency rows, about 7 showed any price text [t08 #2]. A public price is a cheap differentiator.
- Guarantees are almost absent. Only Slopfix showed a warranty in round 1 [t08 #7]. Wavect: "Cancel any week. Last week refunded if we didn't blow you away." [t16][t20 #19]. aibrify: "If we cannot deliver a quoted fix, you do not pay for it." [t16].

### 2.2 Who the $2,000 buyer is (and is not)

- IS: a solo or small-team founder with Stripe and Supabase, real or near-real users, about to launch, raise or sell. Example: the $2,500 childcare-marketplace post: "server-side validation, Stripe production audit, email migration" [t17 J9].
- IS: someone with a backend job left: Twilio and Stripe for a Lovable app ($2,000, Oct 2025); Base44 QA and launch ($2,000, Dec 2025) [t21 #1]. Both old. Budgets, not payments.
- IS NOT: the $20 to $300 poster. That buyer picks the $25 gig [t17 #2].
- Route the under-$1,000 buyer to a free checklist or a "no, sorry". Do not discount to win them. They set the floor [t17 gate 4 note].

### 2.3 What must be true for $2,000 to win over $800 Fiverr/Upwork and over a $2,500 EU studio

Two things. Both must be real before launch.

**Must be true 1: Done is provable without trusting us.**
- The buyer gets written acceptance tests, a security review report, and a handover. They own the repo, hosting and accounts.
- Items: key rotation, row-level security tests, auth checks, Stripe webhook verification, staging vs production split, backup and restore, handover notes [t11 checklist, e.g. item 28 "confirm the client owns Lovable/Supabase/Stripe/domain accounts; document handover"][t19 #4][t20 #18].
- A defined warranty on the listed items (Slopfix: two-week warranty [t08]).
- Why it wins:
  - Against the $800 seller: cheap gigs rarely document a named, verifiable security scope [t11 gate 4]. The buyer with paying users and a Stripe account is the one who cares.
  - Against the EU studio: most studios show no guarantee and no scope document [t08 #7][t21 #8]. GainCafe's "100% success guarantee" is vague [t21 s6]. A clear acceptance test stands out.
- Independent facts that make the risk real in ads and content: 170 of 1,645 Lovable showcase apps had critical access-control failures (CVE-2025-48757) [t01 #4]; UpGuard (Sept 2026) found 16,326 Supabase databases with publicly readable tables (TechCrunch 2026-09-25) [t20 #27]. Platform scanners miss runtime holes [sum gate 5].

**Must be true 2: The risk of choosing us is small and checkable.**
- A paid audit first, credited to the sprint. Buyers and vendors repeat the same advice: a small paid first step with references [t15 #4, #6]. One buyer split a $1,500 budget into a $1,000 audit and a $500 stabilization [t17 J10].
- Two or more named case studies with live URLs and a reference call. Agency guides say video case studies and a reference call matter more than Clutch stars [t15 #7]. Another blog says check "case studies with live URLs and client outcomes" [t08 row 19].
- A stated overlap window. Time-zone overlap is a screening rule for some buyers; 2 to 4 hours is usual, one commenter argues for 4 [t15 #5] (old, weak). My inference, not a track finding: Pakistan (UTC+5) can give a 4-hour window with the UK, the Gulf and US East mornings if the delivery lead runs a shifted day. Confirm Wanile can staff this.
- A public price. The studio-tier buyer compares prices; most sellers hide theirs [t08 #2].

**Not enough on their own:** a lower price, Clutch stars, a badge, "senior engineers" claims. Every seller says these.

**If one of the two fails:** the $2,000 offer loses to the $800 seller on price and to the EU studio on trust. In that case do not cut price. Cut scope or change the buyer.

### 2.4 Positioning lines (one per tier)

- Against the floor: "A $30 gig fixes one bug. We prove the whole app is safe to launch." (Borrow the claim only if the delivery team can back it.)
- Against the studio tier: "Same price band, with the scope, tests and warranty in writing." (Studios show few guarantees [t08 #7].)
- Against the premium tier: "For small apps under about 20 to 30k lines. For bigger codebases we will tell you and point you to the right kind of team." (Fixed-price week is viable only for a small, one-stack app [t19 s162].) Slopfix sells deletion on larger codebases; $10,000 a week is a different product [t08 #5].
- Against both the floor and platforms: "Real engineers, not prompt operators." One PeoplePerHour buyer wrote: "We need somebody that built a clone of one of these two platforms or something similar, not somebody that is using these platforms to vibe code." [t21 buyer quotes, PPH, approximate link]. A single buyer line. Use as a theme, not a claim.

## 3. The Lahore question: show or hide?

### 3.1 Evidence

For "buyers care":
- Old and scattered bias: a Workana post (May 2024) told India and Pakistan applicants not to apply; a Bubble post said Pakistan was fine with a similar time zone [t15 #1]. Weak.
- Blog: clients filter toward US, UK, Eastern European and Indian profiles first, and expect a Pakistan discount; the trust gap is smaller than ten years ago [t15 #3]. Weak.
- One US employee's account (via BusinessWeek, relayed by a vendor, about 2024): outsourced to two Pakistani firms, "the experience was really bad" [t20 #17]. Old, one voice.
- Rival bias: an Indian outsourcer markets "dependable India rather than unsafe Pakistan" [t20 #17].
- HN view of offshore: references from past clients, a small team, a paid trial [t15 #4].

For "buyers hire offshore anyway":
- Pakistani rescue sellers exist at the floor: Fiverr sellers with 60 to 86 reviews (Kamran Asif 86, Ahmed Javed 62 to 75) [t21 #10].
- One Fiverr Lovable-fix gig has 3 reviews, all from US clients [t15 #11].
- Upwork says no restriction stops clients hiring Pakistani freelancers [t15 #2].
- Daily Times calls the "scammer" stereotype a myth, and notes Pakistani firms often work under NDAs that remove branding, so they cannot show work [t20 #17]. That is a proof problem, and we can fix it.

What the big Pakistani firms do:
- Arbisoft lists Plano, Texas first, then Lahore and Islamabad. 10Pearls: Virginia HQ. Tkxel: Clutch primary location Reston, Virginia, with Lahore among 4 locations [t15 #8].
- Pattern: US-first, Pakistan shown but not the headline. Grade moderate for the pattern, via vendor and directory pages. Nothing shows hiding helps [t15 s6].

What is NOT found: any buyer saying they chose or rejected a Pakistani rescuer in 2025-26; whether a US address converts; whether Loom audits, GitHub activity or founder LinkedIn work as trust signals [t15 NOT FOUND][t20 #16].

### 3.2 Stance

**Show it. Plainly. Do not lead with it. Do not hide it. Do not fake a Western address.**

Reasons:
1. It cannot stay hidden. The contract, invoice, Stripe KYC, Clutch page, Upwork profile and Lovable directory all show the company and country. A buyer who finds it late reads "concealment", which is worse than "Lahore". (Inference.)
2. The big Pakistani firms show Pakistan. They lead with a real US office. We have not been told Wanile has one. Do not pretend.
3. The discount expectation is real but small in the evidence. Answer it with proof, not with a hidden address.
4. Showing it supports the strongest trust moves: a reference call, a stated overlap window, named engineers.

How to show it:
- Headline and subhead: no location.
- Above the fold under the CTA: small line "Run by Wanile Technologies, Lahore. 20 engineers. Working hours that overlap with [UK/EU/US East]."
- FAQ answers it directly (section 4, FAQ 1).
- Do not apologise for the price. Do not call it "low cost". Explain price by scope and proof.
- Start the 30-day test in markets where Wanile has references: UK, Australia, Middle East, Europe, US [brief s1]. Time overlap is better with the UK and the Gulf (my inference from UTC+5).

### 4.3 Three symptoms (buyer language)

**1. The fix loop.**
- "a cycle of breakage, each 'fix' resurrecting a previous 'break'" [t02 Q1, B: Bolt user, HN]
- "clicking 'Try to Fix' creates new bugs that trigger more errors, each consuming credits" [t01 S4, S]
- Our line: One person reads the code, finds the cause, adds tests so fixes stay fixed.

**2. The credit bill.**
- "the AI repeated the same coding errors, forcing me to spend thousands of dollars on credits just to resolve the same persistent issues" [t01 s3, B: Trustpilot reviewer, lightly supported]
- "I blew through $70 in a night" [t02 Q4, B: Replit user]
- Our line: A written audit tells you what is broken, what it costs to fix, and whether a rebuild is cheaper.

**3. Works on your screen, not live.**
- "works in preview, fails when deployed" [t21 #12, S]
- Payments do not work in preview; test after deploy; Stripe webhook secret mismatch [t01 #5, S/docs]
- "a live database was readable by anyone with devtools" [t21 s4, S]
- 170 of 1,645 Lovable showcase apps had critical access-control failures [t01 #4, I]
- Our line: We check login, payments, database rules and keys, fix what fails, and show you the tests.

Optional fourth line (nobody understands the code): "can't explain what any of it does" [t19 s4, S].

## 7. What would make me reverse the brand decision

Thresholds are my assumptions to test, not sourced numbers.

**Fold the sub-brand into Wanile (move to A) if:**
1. In the 30-day test, more than half of audit buyers ask "is this Wanile?", or check Wanile's site before booking, and leads that arrive via Wanile's own channels convert at about twice the rate of cold sub-brand leads. Then the Wanile name is the trust asset.
2. The sub-brand cannot get its first 3 reviews or 2 reference calls without using the Wanile name.
3. Wanile's AI-operations pivot stalls and rescue becomes a main revenue line. Then one brand is simpler.
4. Rescue clients move to Wanile retainers at a high rate, so two names confuse the path.
5. The Lovable Expert track is dead, closed, or refuses us, and the "small team" angle has no value. (This removes one reason, not all.)

**Split harder (move to C, hide or cut the link) if:**
6. A security incident, public dispute or chargeback wave hits the sub-brand and the Wanile link spreads it to the main brand. Then cut the link, ring-fence in a separate entity, and keep contracts separate. Check first what contracts and public records already tie the two together.
7. Wanile's SME and enterprise buyers start to ask about the rescue site in sales calls and it lowers perceived price level.
8. A lawyer says the shared contracting entity creates real liability exposure (this belongs in model/risk.md).

**Drop the brand entirely if:**
9. The 30-day test hits the kill criteria in the final report (no paid audits after the set spend). The final verdict may be NO-GO on Gate 1 or Gate 2 [sum].

**Revisit the "show Lahore" stance if:**
10. Three or more prospects drop out at the "are you in Pakistan?" question, or say it in the form or on a call. Then add a real overlap window, a real reference-first step, or a real UK/US contracting entity, before thinking about hiding anything. Hiding stays a last resort.
