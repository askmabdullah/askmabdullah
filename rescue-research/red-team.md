# Red team: the case against "vibe-code rescue" (Phase 4)

Author: red-team agent, 2026-10-21 (run date 2026-10-20). Read: BRIEF.md, verification-log.md, phase1-summary.md, all five model files, tracks 01 to 22, competitors.md, pricing-map.md, ads-library.md. Not read: FINAL-REPORT.md. No web access. Rules: [UNVERIFIED] items are not evidence; [PARAPHRASE, NOT VERBATIM] items are used only as paraphrase; [A], [E], [GK] and [V] figures are assumptions and attacked as such. Pointers: T17 = tracks/17-buyer-voices-round2.md, J9 = job post J9, #7 = key finding 7; model files by section.

My brief is to argue the strongest evidence-based case that this is a crowded, low-budget market that will waste Wanile's time and ad money, and that the GO gates in BRIEF section 5 are not met. I am not neutral. Thin evidence is part of the case: the brief says "Do not force a GO."

## 0. The case in one paragraph

After about 460 searches and zero pages opened (STATUS.md), the research found no buyer who paid a human $1,500 or more to rescue an AI-built app (T17 #1, T22 #1, verification-log). It found no advertiser for this service on any paid channel after 47 targeted searches (T5 #1, T18 #1). The only part of the proposed fix list with a source is the Supabase security core (T11 section 7), which is exactly what Lovable and Replit now scan for in-product (T12 #11, #13). $2,000 is the median, not the top, of at least eight small Western and EU studios already selling the same thing (T21 #7, #8); Pakistani sellers sell it at $17 an hour (T21 #5); buyers who name Lovable on job boards budget $20 to $1,200 (T17 #2). Every number that lets the model pass a gate is tagged [A], [E] or [V]; the verifiers counted about 246 labelled assumptions. Four of five gates are "unclear" in the orchestrator's own read (phase1-summary); the fifth is "unclear, leaning pass". That is not a GO.

## 1. Gate 1 fails: no buyer has been shown paying $1,500 or more

Gate 1 asks for "evidence of buyers paying $1,500 or more for rescue or finishing work". The answer: "No cache entry shows any buyer paying a human $1,500 or more" (verification-log 3). Track 17, 45 searches on this question: "Zero verbatim buyer statements of 'I paid $X to a human to fix my AI-built app' were found" (T17 #1). Track 22, 40 searches on review sites: "not met on this evidence" (T22 section 7). Track 2: "none found" (T2 #10). Track 3: "No buyer payment of any size found" (T3 section 7). What was found instead:

**The two $2,000 posts are old, feature-scoped and seen by title only.** "Twilio and Stripe Integration for Lovable App", $2,000 fixed, posted 2025-10-28, and "Full-Stack Base44 & QA Engineer for App and Website Launch", $2,000 fixed, posted 2025-12-31 (T21 section 3). Both are over nine months old and are budgets: "No proposal counts, hire status or spend shown" (T21 #2). Verifier 3: the first is "add Twilio and Stripe to an app whose UI is done", the second is QA plus a WordPress migration, so "both are finish or launch jobs with feature scope ... weaker rescue evidence than a pure fix job" (verification-log 3). A buyer budgeting $2,000 for a payments backend is under-pricing a build, not paying for a rescue.

**Buyers who name Lovable or "AI-built" budget $20 to $1,200.** J1 $20 (2025-10-08), J2 $30 (2025-10-15), J3 $300 (2025-10-23), J5 $100 (2025-09-01, "max 3-5 hour task", T21 section 3), J6 $1,000 (2026-04-03), J7 $1,200 (2026-10-15) (T17 3A). "Stated budgets cluster at $20 to $300" (T17 #2). A Freelancer.com job to debug vibe-coded apps carried "$100 per project" and drew 153 bidders averaging $96 (T19 #12). That is the clearing price when a buyer says "vibe coded" out loud.

**The two posts at or above $1,500 do not say the app is AI-built.** J9 ($2,500, Upwork, 2026-10-12, a solo founder's childcare marketplace on Supabase and Stripe) and J10 ($1,500, Freelancer.com, $1,000 audit plus $500 stabilisation) are the model's favourite buyers (offers.md 3, brand.md 2.2). Track 17: "AI-built NOT stated" for both (T17 3A). The one verified buyer quote on price is J10's: "I understand $1,500 may not cover every fix." (T17 3B). That is a buyer apologising for a $1,500 ceiling, read as support for a $2,500 sprint.

**The loudest voices are sellers.** Every $1,500-plus price in tracks 8, 10, 16, 18, 19 and 21 is an asking price: Slopfix $10,000 a week with "no named clients" (T19 #9), McKelvey $25K to $50K on "a seller's own page" (T18 #8). The one marketplace built for this niche shows the ratio: VibeCodeFixers signed up 300 to 600 experts and matched 30 to 40 projects, "Supply side outnumbers demand side by 10x or more" (T19 #11). 404 Media "could not confirm that LinkedIn 'vibe coding cleanup specialists' actually earn money from it" (T10 #4). The brief's four Reddit voices were "NOT found" (T10 #7).

**The Clutch reviews have no cost band.** Track 22 found three reviews of the right shape (GeekyAnts, Connectish, Cleveroad): "ZERO reviews with a verified cost band" (T22 section 1). Connectish's is about Oct 2024, outside the 12-month window (T22 R2); GeekyAnts' profile floor is $10,000 or more (T22 #3), five times Wanile's price. Gate 1 rests on fields nobody has read.

**The remaining buyer-side prices are small, and the pool that could pay is unmeasured.** $600 for a two-day fix, secondhand via a vendor blog (T10 section 3); a live cafe app owner offering a flat $120 a month for on-call RLS fixes, 2026-10-14 (T17 J11); an Indie Hackers founder, after "20 breaks", seeking a developer with "not a lot of money -- if any budgeted" (T22 #13, paraphrase). The 80% non-technical figure is Lovable's own unaudited survey, and "non-technical" means role, not budget (T12 #4). BigIdeasDB, the only revenue-verified sample, says 7.6% of vibe-coded startups report any MRR and none cleared $1,000 in the last 30 days (T13 #12). A buyer whose app makes under $1,000 a month and whose tool stack costs $91 to $131 a month (T14 #7) is the $120-a-month buyer in J11, not a $2,500 buyer.

Gate 1 verdict: FAIL. The brief asked for payment evidence. There is none. Confirmed AI-built budgets top out at $1,200; the $2,000 posts are stale builds; the $1,500 and $2,500 posts are not AI-built; the reviews have no band.

## 2. Gate 2 fails: no channel has a measured cost per client

Gate 2 asks for a channel with "a plausible cost per paying client under 30% of the first project's value", about $600. The channel economist's first line: "nothing here is a measured cost per client" (channels.md "How to read this file").

**Meta: zero advertisers, and the model's own mid case fails by 6x.** Track 5 (14 searches) and track 18 (33) both report "0" advertisers evidenced on any paid channel (T5 #1, T18 #1; ads-library.md Table A is empty). The brief's claim that agencies "spend heavily on Meta ads ... and close well" gets "unsupported by any evidence found. Not refuted either" (channels.md 5). Lovable itself is the heavy Meta advertiser here, 1,079 ads and 304 active in one snapshot (T18 #5), bidding for the same audience.

The funnel model stitches vendor benchmarks from other niches (all [V], including a restaurant-marketing cost per booked call) onto two inputs with no source at all: lead-to-booked-call 40 to 60% [A] and audit-to-sprint 20 to 50% [A] (channels.md 1.1, 1.2); the benchmarks "do not reconcile with each other" (channels.md 1.1). Output: cost per sprint client $676 gross in High, $4,143 gross ($3,666 net) in Mid, $20,833 in Low: "fails 6x" at Mid, "fails 33x" at Low; only the High case, needing a $50 B2B CPL and a 50% audit-to-sprint rate, both unsupported, gets near the line (channels.md 1.3, 1.5). "At a $2,000 ticket only the High case is near a pass ... At $10,000 to $15,000 ... the Mid case would pass" (channels.md 1.4). If the Meta rumour is true at all, it is true for $15K sellers like McKelvey, not for a $2,000 sprint.

The two Phase 2 files also disagree. The offer designer caps acquisition at about $390 per paid audit at a 40% [A] audit-to-sprint rate, $300 at 25% (offers.md 9). The channel model's Mid cost per paid audit is $1,450 (channels.md 1.3): 3.7 to 4.8 times the cap. And the proposed $2,500 test cannot settle it: "$2,500 cannot prove a cost per sprint client" (channels.md 6.3); in Mid it buys 1.7 paid audits, with an 18% chance of none.

**The "cheap" channels pass only on unsourced inputs and cannot fill a calendar.** Platform directories pass at $425 Mid only because inquiries per month (8 / 3 / 1) and inquiry-to-client rate (30 / 20 / 10%) are [A] (channels.md 3.3); Upwork passes at $470 Mid only because jobs per month (15 / 6 / 2) and win rate (12 / 6 / 2%) are [A] (channels.md 3.4). Verifier 3: these passes "rest on [A] inputs ... and not on sourced data." The economist concedes "Confidence is LOW for every row" (channels.md 4). Even at face value the volume is "about 0.4 to 0.6 clients a month" combined (channels.md 3.2): a fraction of one sprint a month for a 20-engineer company.

The directory evidence underneath is weaker still. Contra's Replit page shows 234 hires across 171 experts, 1.4 per expert over an unknown period (T20 #1). Lovable's own pages conflict on whether the Expert programme exists ("is no longer active", T20 #7), and it is capped at teams under five people (T9 #2), so Wanile would need a sub-brand and Lovable's written permission (brand.md 1.7). The most-hired Lovable expert is the cheapest, Weekend Inc, 100 hires at $50 an hour (T20 #8). On Upwork, proposal counts were "not visible" (T21 section 1); the glimpses are 10 to 15 proposals on a $30 job (T7 section 5) and 153 bidders on a $100 job (T19 #12), against a new account with no reviews and $17-an-hour rivals (T21 #5).

**The rest are slower or riskier.** SEO: 7 of 9 results for "vibe code cleanup service" are agency pages (T6 #11) and the model says "not in year one" (channels.md 4). Outbound: CAN-SPAM has no B2B exemption and penalties up to $53,088 per email (T20 #21), CASL and the Spam Act need role-relevant consent (T20 #23, #24), reply rates are 3 to 6% before any call (T20 #25), the "I found a vulnerability" hook reads as extortion (T20 #26); modelled cost $4,724 per client at Mid (channels.md 4). Communities: Reddit returned zero pages in every round (T17, T19, T20, T22), so the channel sellers credit most is the one the research could not observe.

The economist's last word: "None is proven" (channels.md 4). Gate 2 verdict: FAIL. The scalable channels fail by 6x or more on the model's own mid inputs; the channels that pass on paper do so on [A] inputs and deliver under one client a month.

## 3. Gate 3 fails as stated: a repeatable security sublist is not a repeatable rescue

Gate 3 asks whether "most rescues on our stack share the same fix list". Verified position: "the security and data-access hardening list is sourced; 29 of 38 checklist items in track 11 are engineering judgment [E]" (verification-log). Track 11 itself says "NO published rescuer evidence that the same ten problems recur across whole rescues, and no fix-effort data" (T11 section 7). The delivery planner's 58-item list has 37 [E]: "Most of the checklist is still engineering judgment" (delivery.md 3).

**No fix-time data exists.** "No published hours-per-task table exists (t19 #1, t11 #15). We checked." (delivery.md). The only fragments are "2-4 hours" for five patterns from a scanner seller and "about 2 hours" for a key exposure from a vendor sample (T19 #1). Every hour band in delivery.md is "OUR ESTIMATE, NOT PUBLISHED DATA".

**The planner's own estimate breaks the $2,000 week.** Week-one hardening is about 46 engineer hours with AI help, range 25 to 70; "A 40-hour week does not fit that safely"; "A typical Lovable app with Stripe is 2 sprint-weeks" at about 66 hours; the full list is about 100 hours, "3 sprint-weeks" (delivery.md 3). So the $2,000-a-week unit prices a typical app at $4,000 to $6,000 before anything goes wrong, above every confirmed AI-built buyer budget (T17 #2). The offer designer concedes it by pricing the finish sprint at $4,500 and calling it "2.25 times" the two $2,000 posts, "the largest price-gap risk in the ladder" (offers.md 5).

**Repeatable problem classes are not repeatable fixes.** Only secrets and access control are named by 5 of 9 sellers; payments and error handling by 3; tests and fake auth by 2; staging split by 1 (delivery.md 3, correcting T19 #4). Variance inside a class is enormous: Cleveroad found "more than 600 untyped shortcuts in one repository against two in the other" (T22 R3). Base44 exports only the frontend, so rescue there "means a backend rebuild" (T2 #5); Replit locks in its backend (T2 section 6); Lovable Cloud needs a manual migration with a password reset email to every user (T20 #4), "12 to 30 hours, our guess" (delivery.md F5). Three of the brief's five platforms do not share the fourth's fix list.

**Rescues become rewrites at an unknown rate.** "No hard rule, no share of jobs that became rebuilds" (T19 #5). Both HN freelancers who described a cleanup job ended in a rewrite: "I ended up recommending that I just rewrite the whole codebase" and "hired me to assess it, and ultimately rewrite it" (T19 section 4). The only triage thresholds are "vendor heuristics, not research" (T19 #5a).

**"Finish my app", the thing buyers ask for, is a $15K to $50K product elsewhere.** Sellers who finish apps charge "$15,000 to $50,000 over 4 to 8 weeks" (offers.md 5, citing T19 #2, #13, T16 #8); agencies estimate "6 to 8 weeks for a 12.7k-line Next.js plus Stripe app" (T19 #2); the HN "100 hour gap" post puts a simple app at about 100 hours after the prototype (T19 #3). Track 19: "Do NOT promise 'finish the app'". Yet the two $2,000 posts gate 1 leans on are finish jobs, and the hero headline in brand.md 4.1 is "We finish it and prove it works."

**The scope-creep controls are untested guesses.** The code-freeze and change-control ideas "came from the search tool's own synthesis, not from a source. So this whole section is [E]" (delivery.md 6), and the freeze depends on Lovable's GitHub sync: "We have not verified this" (delivery.md Control 1).

Gate 3 verdict: FAIL as written. Supported only for a security sublist that platforms are automating (section 5), with no hours, no rebuild rate, and a typical app that needs two to three "one-week" units.

## 4. Gate 4 fails: $2,000 is the median of the crowd, not a position above the floor

"$2,000 is the median of the studio tier, not above it. At least 8 small vendors already list there" (brand.md 2.1). The eleven fixed-rescue "from" prices run $1,400 to $10,000 with a median of $2,500 (T21 section 5): Teyrex $1,400, Base44Devs $1,500, Vibe Code Rescue $1,500, AfterBuild $1,999, Arkroot EUR 1,999, GainCafe $2,500, Forasoft $2,500. Track 21: "The $2,000 to $2,500 per week unit is no longer an empty slot" (T21 #8). The differentiators the model relies on are already in market: Wavect refunds the last week, aibrify does pay-only-if-fixed, GainCafe claims a "100% success guarantee", TopDevz and Vibe Code Rescue credit the audit (T16 #1, #3, #4; T21 #7). Free 48-hour audits are "the observed norm" (T20 #19), so a $495 paid audit from Lahore competes with free ones from named Western agencies.

Below the band, the floor is Pakistani. A Bahawalnagar seller offers "Vibe Code Rescue" with a written audit first at $17 an hour; XCONS Solutions in Lahore sells vibe code rescue on its own site; Fiverr rescue gigs run $40 to $100 (T21 #5, competitors.md D). "A Lahore studio at $2,000 would be above every Pakistani price seen" (competitors.md D) is the problem, not the opportunity: buyers who search "Pakistan" and "Lovable fix" see $17 an hour first, and advice blogs say buyers "expect a Pakistan discount" (T15 #3, weak). Forty hours of the Lovable directory's top-hired expert at $50 an hour (T20 #8) is $2,000: the "fixed week" is the cheapest credible hourly rate repackaged.

Above the band, an EU studio at $2,500 has the trust advantage: Arkroot (Germany) at EUR 1,999, Connectish (Germany) with a Clutch review for a Bolt rebuild, Wavect (Austria), Pragmatic Coders (Poland) (T21 #7, T22 R2, T8 #10). "Against a $2,500 EU studio, $2,000 is only 20% cheaper ... A 20% gap will not cover the trust gap" (brand.md 2.1). The trust evidence is thin but all one way: a 2024 job post told India and Pakistan applicants not to apply (T15 #1), and "No 2025-26 buyer voice about Pakistan for rescue work was found" (T20 #16). Wanile's Clutch count is "unknown here" (brand.md 8). Track 17's gate 4 read is "contradicts in part". Gate 4 verdict: FAIL. The position exists; it is crowded, undercut from Pakistan and out-trusted from the EU.

## 5. Gate 5 collides with gate 3: the only sourced work is the work platforms are automating

Track 12's reading: "The easiest-to-productise rescue items (RLS, secrets, dependency and config holes) are the exact items platforms are now scanning for. A security-only audit is the part most likely to be commoditised first" (T12 section 6). That security core is the only part of gate 3 with a source. Lovable ships dependency auditing, AI code review, RLS analysis and database config checks, plus a Wiz integration on 2026-05-07 (T12 #11). Replit shipped a Security Agent on 2026-04-21 and black-box penetration testing with agent remediation on 2026-08-17, "a new security layer every 3 to 4 months" (T12 #13). Lovable Cloud, on by default since 2025-09-29, "removes the 'wire up Supabase and auth yourself' failure for new apps" (T12 #12, T20 #3).

Platforms are also intermediating the hire. Base44 has an in-product marketplace with partners at $10 to $100 an hour and a possible 15% cut (T9 #6); Replit's expert links redirect to Contra at $50 to $75 an hour (T20 #1); Lovable's directory's most-hired expert charges $50 an hour (T20 #8). Track 20: "the platforms are intermediating, not delivering ... Risk remains that the Lovable partner directory captures lead flow." A platform that owns the stuck builder's screen, the scan report and the hire button sets the price, and it is setting it by the hour at $50.

The buyer is also learning to self-serve. An HN commenter said a $200 subscription gets similar value to Slopfix's $10,000 week (T21 #13, paraphrase); another says clients now fix small bugs with AI and call a developer only for harder problems (T14 #10); track 14 grades this "weakly contradicts" gate 5. The segment is 80% non-technical by Lovable's own survey and pays $91 to $131 a month for its whole stack (T14 #7). Risk.md 5.5: "the audit gets commoditised first." Gate 5 verdict: UNCLEAR at best. What survives in 12 months is the unsourced, unpriced, rebuild-prone part.

## 6. Unit economics and opportunity cost: a 20-engineer firm chasing $495 audits

The audit, the funnel's front door, loses money once loaded: $495 price, 35% gross margin loaded, 23% expected, minus 24% in the stress case, "a qualification product, not a profit product" (offers.md 3). The healthy-looking sprint margins rest on a lead reviewer at $2,400 to $2,500 a month [A] and a loading multiplier of 1.5 to 1.75 [A]; "If the true loaded multiplier is 2.5, B's expected margin falls to about 50%" (offers.md 12). Hours, overrun and refund rates are all [A] (offers.md 1).

Capacity, not cash, is the real cost. "A 3x job holds an engineer for 3 weeks and sells one week. That is about $4,000 of work not sold" (delivery.md 4). Break-even for a two-engineer pod is about two sprint-weeks a month; "If we sell nothing, the cost is still $2,000 to $2,200 a month" (delivery.md 4). Each $2,000 sale carries the sales and delivery motion of a $20,000 one (offers.md 3, 4; delivery.md 1, 7); the kill rule budgets "30 qualified fit calls" to find three paid audits (offers.md 11).

Lifetime value cannot rescue the arithmetic because retention is 0% proven. "No source reports a rescue-to-retainer rate. Two rounds found no buyer voice on keeping a developer" (offers.md 8, T14 #8, T20 #11). At 0% attach the 3-to-1 rule caps acquisition at $441, "stricter than gate 2" (channels.md 2.4). The one buyer-side maintenance price is $120 a month (T17 J11); the $450 Care plan is "3.4 to 5 times their tool bill" (offers.md 8).

Now the opportunity cost. Wanile has about 20 engineers, has already done a paid audit plus fix contract for an AI SaaS and rebuilt a legal AI platform, and its brand "is moving upmarket to AI operations and AI-native engineers" (BRIEF 1, Q10). The research's anchors for that work: a dedicated Pakistan-based developer at $4,000 to $6,400 a month (T20 #15), fractional CTO at $5,000 to $25,000 (T14 #6). One dedicated-engineer placement at the model's own $3,800 (offers.md 8) earns $45,600 a year from one sale; matching it with $2,000 sprints needs about 23 sales a year, each with its own acquisition cost and dispute exposure. The channel economist admits the one asset that could sell either product is not in the model: "Existing Wanile relationships ... are not modelled ... Asking existing clients for introductions is the most likely cheapest channel of all" (channels.md 4). Those introductions are finite. Spending them on $495 audits for pre-revenue founders instead of AI-operations retainers is the most expensive line in this plan, and it appears in no table.

## 7. Research quality: this base cannot carry a GO, and only just carries a NO-GO

Every fact came through one path: WebSearch snippets and tool-written summaries. "22 track files, about 460 searches, 0 pages opened" (STATUS.md). "Verified" means "faithfully transcribed from search output, not confirmed against the original page" (verification-log). Round 1 gave 13 of 16 tracks only 7 to 20 searches before a shared quota cut them off (STATUS.md). After verification, track 3 has zero verbatim buyer quotes (verification-log 1), and the brand agent counts "about 7 usable fragments" of buyer language in all tracks, "none ... from a buyer who paid a human" (brand.md 0).

The sources the brief said to "read deeply" were never read. Seventeen subreddits were named (BRIEF 6); across about 20 Reddit-targeted queries in track 17 alone "ZERO Reddit pages (or Reddit mirror pages) came back" (T17 section 1), and the same held in tracks 19, 20 and 22; r/lovable, about 51k members (T14 #13), is invisible to this run. The Ad Library, Google Trends, Fiverr tiers, Upwork proposal counts and the three Clutch "Project size" fields were never opened (T5, T6 #15, T7 #9, T22 section 1). Errors reached the summary layer: "5 to 10% with any revenue" dropped its "my arithmetic" label and the "none cleared $1,000" caveat, and Reeve's vendor scan was called "independent" (verification-log 2, failures 7 and 8).

So the record cannot support a GO: gate 1 needs a payment and there is none; gate 2 needs a measurement and there is none. Can it support a confident NO-GO? Only partly: the biggest absences are tool limits, and the J9 buyer ($2,500, 2026-10-12) is real and the right shape. A confident NO-GO needs the same evidence looked for properly and still absent. The research would need to have found, and did not: (1) five named buyers in the last 12 months who paid $1,500 or more to a human to rescue an AI-built app (Clutch project-size fields, Upwork hire status, first-person Reddit threads); (2) three or more rescue advertisers in the UK or EU Ad Library running 60-plus days, or one seller post-mortem with a cost per call; (3) a practitioner hours log or three case studies with hours per fix and a rescue-to-rebuild share; (4) proposal counts and hire status on the two $2,000 posts; (5) one 2025-26 buyer explaining a choice for or against an offshore rescuer; (6) a rescue-to-retainer rate from any practitioner. None needs more than a browser and a day. Until then the honest call is NO-GO on the evidence as it stands, not GO on evidence that might exist.

## 8. Brand, reputation, refunds and liability

Wanile's main brand "is moving upmarket to AI operations and AI-native engineers" (BRIEF Q10). The brand agent's own warning: "The SME ops buyer sees 'fix your Lovable app' and reads 'junior shop'" (brand.md 1.3). The sub-brand does not sever the link: footer, About page, contract, invoice and card descriptor all name Wanile Technologies, Lahore, and "if Wanile signs, Wanile carries the liability" (brand.md 1.6). A cleanup pitch on HN was called "a thin advertorial" (T19 section 4) and security-flavoured outreach is seen as "a nuisance" or extortion (T20 #26). The ad angle "Is your database open to anyone with a browser?" (brand.md 5.3) is fear marketing for a service whose contract must say "We do not certify the app as secure" (risk.md 1.6).

The buyers are refund-prone by construction: non-technical, having "lost money to platform credits and freelancers, and learned that refunds are denied", so "more likely to use their card issuer as the refund channel" (risk.md 2.1, an inference, since "There is NO evidence in the tracks of rescue chargebacks"). Lovable's Trustpilot reviewers report "thousands of dollars on credits" and refunds refused (T1 #3). Hardening work is invisible: "I paid and nothing changed that I can see"; "fix" is heard as "finish" (risk.md 2.2), and finishing is a $15K product. "At our volume of a few payments a month, one dispute is a large rate" (risk.md 1.11, [GK]).

Liability is the tail risk. Lovable's position on the CVE is that "each individual customer of the Lovable platform accepts a responsibility over protecting the data of their application" (T11 #1); the customer will pass that to whoever touched the code last. A Replit agent deleted a production database with 1,206 executives' records (T11 #11). A breach claim "can be far bigger" than a $2,000 fee (risk.md 1.9). Pakistan has no EU or UK adequacy decision, so each EU or UK client needs SCCs or an IDTA and a transfer risk assessment (risk.md 1.7, [GK]); "No premium or availability data for a Pakistan-based firm" was found for E&O or cyber cover (risk.md 3.3); the contract needs multi-country lawyer review before first use (risk.md 7). The legal set-up alone could exceed the gross profit of the first several sprints.

## 9. Verdict

**NO-GO**, on gates 1 and 2, with gates 3 and 4 failing as written and gate 5 unclear. No buyer has been shown paying $1,500 or more; no channel has a measured cost per client; the only sourced work is being automated; the price sits in a crowded band; and the plan spends Wanile's scarce introductions and senior attention on $495 audits while the company moves upmarket.

The narrowest version I would tolerate testing, and only after two half-days of browser work (open the three Clutch pages and the UK/EU Ad Library; if the bands are under $1,500 and the advertiser count is zero, stop):
- No new brand, no ad spend, no landing page, no Lovable Expert application. Wanile's own name and existing clients only.
- One offer: a paid "launch hardening" engagement at $1,500 to $2,500 on Supabase plus Stripe apps, sold to the J9-shaped buyer whether or not the app was AI-built, the only buyer shape that posted a budget at that level (T17 J9, J10).
- One engineer part-time plus the lead, 60 days, sourced from existing-client introductions, Wanile's existing Upwork or Clutch history, and at most 20 Upwork proposals.
- Hard kill criteria, any one of which ends the test: fewer than 3 paid engagements at $1,500 or more from at least 20 qualified conversations by day 60; any chargeback or refund dispute; engineer hours above 1.5x the audit estimate on 2 of the first 3 jobs; any "rebuild" verdict sold as a sprint; any existing-client introduction that would otherwise have gone to an AI-operations conversation.
