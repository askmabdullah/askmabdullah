## Q1. Where is ownership change most active in 2025-2026? (deal volume, by industry and deal type)

### Takeaway
Ownership change in US small and mid-size businesses runs at a very high, steady pace. Add-ons are about three of every four US buyouts, which is about 5,500 add-on deals a year (estimate). SBA 7(a) acquisition loans run at about 6,000 a year (estimate), and about 9,600 Main Street businesses were reported sold in 2025. The fastest-growing waves in 2025-2026 are PE buying accounting firms and residential/home services. Dental, insurance-agency and MSP deal volume is still large but cooled in 2025.

### Cited Findings

**US private equity overall**
- Q3 2026 US PE: 2,467 deals worth $233.8B, per a law-firm summary of PitchBook's Q3 2026 US PE Breakdown. A Foley summary of the PitchBook Q3 2026 Global PE First Look gives 2,466 deals, up 5.8% quarter on quarter. Range: 2,466-2,467. — [CLA](https://www.claconnect.com/en/resources/blogs/private-equity/larger-deals-lift-us-private-equity-but-selectivity-still-matters); [Foley](https://www.foley.com/?p=129506)
- **Add-ons were 74.9% of US buyout transactions through Q3 2026.** Growth/expansion equity was 24.8% of total PE deal count, a record annual share if it holds. The summary does not say whether the add-on figure is by count or by value. — [CLA summary of PitchBook](https://www.claconnect.com/en/resources/blogs/private-equity/larger-deals-lift-us-private-equity-but-selectivity-still-matters)
- Q2 2026 US PE deal count: 2,384 (PitchBook, via SGA newsletter). — [SGA Analytics Newsletter Issue 31, Aug 2026](https://www.sganalytics.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/SGA_Newsletter_Issue-31.pdf)
- **Estimate (math shown):** If roughly 75% of quarterly PE deal count is buyouts (100% minus the 24.8% growth equity share), then buyouts ≈ 2,466 × 0.75 ≈ 1,850 a quarter. Add-ons ≈ 1,850 × 0.749 ≈ 1,385 a quarter, or **about 5,500 US add-on acquisitions a year**. This is an estimate. It assumes the add-on share is by count and that all non-growth deals are buyouts.

### Inferences
- **Serial acquirers turn "one-off integration" into a weekly pipeline.** Apex at about 60 add-ons a year is about 5 a month. Southern Veterinary Partners at 139 since 2021 is about 28 a year, or 2-3 a month (estimate, assuming roughly 5 years). Evergreen at 23 over 3 years is about 8 a year. For a platform at that pace, integration work (data migration, chart-of-accounts mapping, payer enrollment, client letters, pricebook loading) happens continuously. That makes it pass the recurrence test at platform level even though it fails at single-deal level.
- Hottest by growth rate: PE in accounting firms (about 45x in deal count from 2019 to 2025) and residential services (2025 second-highest on record). Large but cooling: dental (down 37% in 2025), insurance agencies (down 12%). The steady base: SBA change-of-ownership (about 6,100 a year), search funds (about 860 cumulative, with acquisition rates falling to about 48%), and independent sponsors (1,200-1,400).
- Sizes: Main Street deals (median price $350K) are too small to pay for much outside help. Search fund and SBA deals at $1M-$16M price and $0.5M-$2.5M EBITDA can pay $1K-$5K a month. Lower-middle-market PE platforms with 10-60 sites can pay $5K-$25K a month (inference from fractional CFO pricing below).

## Q2. What recurring operational pain appears after the deal? (problem records with the six-test screen)

### Takeaway
Seven recurring problems and one near-miss came through this lens. The strongest are (1) monthly multi-entity close and chart-of-accounts mapping at roll-up platforms, (2) payer re-credentialing at healthcare roll-ups, and (3) the first accrual close and investor pack for first-time owners (search fund, SBA and ETA buyers). Fund-level KPI collection is real but is drawing well-funded AI competition. Covenant and lender reporting carries a clear Shariah question. Earnout tracking mostly fails the weekly test.

### Cited Findings

#### P1. Roll-up multi-entity monthly close and chart-of-accounts mapping (platform level)
- **One line:** Each acquired site arrives with its own ledger (QuickBooks, Xero, Sage) and its own chart of accounts. Every month someone has to map, reconcile, eliminate and consolidate them into site and group P&Ls before the sponsor's deadline.
- **Industry / deal type:** PE buy-and-build platforms in home services, vet, dental, MSPs, CPA firms and insurance agencies. Search-fund buy-and-builds are also affected.
- **Size where it hurts most:** Platforms with 5-60 entities and $10M-$150M revenue, before ERP migration. These are too big for one bookkeeper and too small for a full FP&A team (inference).
- **The task in plain words:** Pull each entity's trial balance monthly; map accounts to the platform chart of accounts; fix miscoded items; book intercompany eliminations; produce a consolidated pack, site benchmarking and variance commentary; chase site managers for missing data.
- **Evidence of pain:**
  - "The decision matters most at the first add-on, when a second chart of accounts arrives" (paraphrase of the search summary). Reporting gets harder with "more companies, more add-ons, more reporting requests". — [SaaS BPM](https://saasbpm.com/portfolio-company-reporting-automation-that-survives-a-board-cycle/) (vendor); [Planr](https://planr.com/10-ways-spreadsheets-break-pe-portfolio-reporting/) (vendor)
  - The monthly process is described as "collect, copy, clean, reconcile, chase, format, check, and recheck". Metric definitions drift across companies, and spreadsheet versions multiply. — [Planr](https://planr.com/10-ways-spreadsheets-break-pe-portfolio-reporting/) (vendor claim)
  - Article title: "Board reporting for portfolio companies: why the monthly pack takes six people and still ships wrong numbers". The page was blocked, so only the title was seen. — [Growth Shuttle](https://growthshuttle.com/board-reporting-for-portfolio-companies-why-the-monthly-pack-takes-six-people-and-still-ships-wrong-numbers/)
  - A vet group cut monthly close from up to 23 days to 6-7 days after moving to Sage Intacct (vendor claim). — [BTerrell (Sage Intacct partner)](https://www.bterrell.com/industry/veterinary)
  - Dental groups lose close time on collections reconciliation, provider-pay accruals, intercompany charges and the consolidation workbook (vendor). — [Sage blog](https://www.sage.com/en-us/blog/dental-group-month-end-close-replacing-consolidation-spreadsheet/) (blocked; search summary only)
- **Frequency / hours:** Monthly close, plus weekly flash reports in many sponsor-backed companies. The weekly-flash point is an inference: my search on it was cut off by the budget. I found no sourced hours figure; the "six people" claim is a vendor-blog title only.
- **What they pay today:**
  - Fractional CFO: $6K-$12K a month for $5M-$15M revenue (15-25 hours); $12K-$20K for $15M-$50M (25-40 hours). — [Acuity](https://acuity.co/fractional-cfo/) (vendor). JRG Partners: $8K-$16K "standard", $15K-$25K+ "heavy". — [JRG Partners](https://www.jrgpartners.com/?p=80073) (vendor)
  - Integration staff: one VP M&A Integration posting at a PE-backed industrial services platform offers $230K-$280K base plus bonus and equity. Salary.com estimates $205K-$286K in Chicago. One posting is a "newly created position, reporting directly to the Operating Partner, responsible for post-acquisition integration across the portfolio". — [JRG Partners job posting, Chicago](https://www.jrgpartners.com/jobs/vp-ma-integration-private-equity-group-chicago-il/) (the $230K-$280K figure came from one recruiter posting in the same results set; its exact URL is uncertain)
  - Mid-market FP&A suites: Vendr medians Datarails $33,300/yr, Vena $34,286, Planful $42,403, plus 25%-100% of year-one subscription for implementation. — [Aleph (competitor) citing Vendr](https://www.getaleph.com/answers/fpa-software-pricing-guide-2026). Datarails' own guide says $35K-$80K/yr. — [Datarails](https://www.datarails.com/datarails-cost-and-features/) (vendor)
  - Outsourced multi-entity accounting firms advertise close in 5-7 days and 70%-85% savings versus in-house. — [Certum Solutions](https://www.certumsolutions.com/all-services) (vendor claim). Pricing is quote-based; no per-entity price was found.
- **Cheap-tool check (test f):**
  - Fathom (USD): Starter $53, Silver $280 (10 entities), Gold $400 (25), Platinum $720 (50). Multi-entity consolidation is included at every tier and can cover up to 300 entities on different accounting systems. — [Eightx review 2026](https://eightx.co/blog/compare/reviews/fathom-for-ecommerce-review); [ERPResearch](https://erpresearch.com/erp-add-ons/reporting-tools/fathom/pricing)
  - Reach Reporting: $149 (1 connection), $290 (10), $550 (25), $950 (50) a month, with multi-currency consolidation and eliminations. — [Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/product/148141-Reach-Reporting/); [Reach FAQ](https://reachreporting.com/faq-page)
  - Verdict: the consolidation engine costs $280-$950 a month, which is above the $100 bar but cheap. These tools assume clean, mapped ledgers. Fathom's own support docs say you must first import each entity before building a group. — [Fathom support](https://support.fathomhq.com/en/articles/999174). The mapping, clean-up, chasing and commentary are not done by the tool. Not checked: Intuit Enterprise Suite multi-entity pricing (search budget gone).
- **Six tests:** a pass (monthly, plus weekly flash); b pass (fractional CFO $6K-$25K a month, integration hires above $200K); c pass; d pass (platform CFO, with operating partner sign-off); e pass (AI can map charts of accounts, flag anomalies and draft commentary; a human controller reviews and signs); f pass with a warning (Fathom and Reach make consolidation itself cheap, so the service must sell the mapped, checked close, not dashboards). **Result: 6/6.**
- **AI over 24 months:** Mixed. Better models make chart-of-accounts mapping and anomaly detection cheap for a service provider, which helps margins. They also help Fathom, Reach and AI-native ERPs build mapping in, so the stand-alone tooling gap shrinks. A sponsor still wants a named human checking the number (inference). Add-on volume (about 75% of buyouts) keeps the work flowing.
- **How to reach buyers:** ACG chapters, platform CFOs at add-on-heavy sponsors, and Fathom and Reach partner directories (both sell through accountants). Not verified: specific partner-programme terms.
- **Count:** About 5,500 US add-ons a year (estimate above). Roughly 130 PE-backed DSOs; 95 active insurance-agency buyers in 2025; 386 PE-backed CPA deals since 2020. I found no platform census.
- **Strongest argument against:** Crowded by outsourced accounting/CAS firms, which PE is itself rolling up (179 deals in 2025), and offshore BPOs. Platforms migrate to one ERP within 12-24 months, so the mapping pain is temporary for each entity. It persists only while add-ons keep arriving.
- **Halal flag:** Most clients are leveraged buyout platforms with interest-bearing acquisition debt. The service is to the operating company, not arranging debt. Flag for review.

#### P2. Healthcare change-of-ownership payer re-credentialing and enrollment tracking
- **One line:** After a practice is bought, the clinicians must be re-enrolled with Medicare, Medicaid and commercial payers under the new owner's entity or TIN. Claims are denied or held until this is done, often for months.
- **Industry / deal type:** PE-backed dental DSOs, physical therapy and rehab, dermatology, behavioral health, home health, ASCs and physician groups. Not vet, which has no payer enrollment.
- **Size:** Platforms closing several add-ons a month (for example, Specialized Dental at 55 add-ons and Ivy Rehab at 24 since 2021). It is also acute for single-practice buyers who lack credentialing staff.
- **The task:** Build provider rosters; file payer applications or change-of-ownership notices; follow up weekly by portal and phone; track effective dates; flag claims at risk; keep CAQH and payer records current.
- **Evidence:**
  - Failing to plan "can halt your practice's cash flow for 90-120 days post-sale". In the example, commercial claims were denied after closing because providers were not enrolled under the new entity. — [Trufort Business Group case study](https://trufortebusinessgroup.com/case-study-credentialing-problems-before-closing/) / [SovDoc](https://sovdoc.com/glossary/credentialing) (the search summary did not say which; both are advisory or vendor pages)
  - A home-health seller that started payer applications after closing needed about 90 extra days. This led to a larger escrow holdback and a worse structure for the seller. — [Trufort](https://trufortebusinessgroup.com/case-study-credentialing-problems-before-closing/)
  - Sale of a physician practice triggers new identification numbers, and physicians must be re-credentialed, which "can take several months". Medicare change of ownership is an application and approval process with CMS, not an automatic transfer. — [Becker's ASC](https://www.beckersasc.com/asc-accreditation-and-patient-safety/4-tips-to-addressing-physician-credentialing-with-payers-during-an-acquisition.html)
  - In one DSO, payer contracts tied to a single TIN meant that changing the TIN changed network status for every practice under it. — [SovDoc](https://sovdoc.com/glossary/credentialing) (search summary)
- **Dollar cost (estimate, math shown):** A practice billing $2M a year to insured payers has about $167K a month of receipts at risk. A 90-120-day delay holds back $500K-$667K of cash ($2M ÷ 12 × 3 to 4) even if it is eventually paid. Timely-filing denials become permanent losses. This is an estimate, not a sourced figure.
- **Who feels it / approves:** VP revenue cycle or credentialing manager; the CFO approves. Integration leads also own it during each deal.
- **Today:** In-house credentialing specialists or outsourced credentialing firms. I found no prices.
- **Cheap-tool check:** CAQH ProView is free for providers (prior knowledge, not verified this session) but does not file payer change-of-ownership applications. Credentialing software exists; I did not find its prices.
- **Six tests:** a pass at platform level (several add-ons a month plus new hires; weekly follow-ups), fail for a one-time buyer; b pass (cash-flow freeze, escrow impact); c pass; d pass (CFO); e partial pass (AI drafts forms, tracks status and prepares calls; payer portals and phone follow-up still need humans); f provisional pass (no sub-$100 tool found). **Result: 6/6, provisional.**
- **AI over 24 months:** It grows while healthcare roll-ups continue (about 747 healthcare services deals in 2025). AI agents get better at portal work, but payer processes stay slow and messy, so the human-checker share stays meaningful (inference).
- **How to reach buyers:** DSO and MSO revenue-cycle leaders (DSO events; not verified this session), healthcare M&A attorneys and buy-side advisers who see the 90-day gap at closing.
- **Count:** About 130 PE-backed DSOs; about 747 healthcare services deals in 2025.
- **Against:** It overlaps the "paperwork" and "bad vertical software" lenses. Specialist credentialing vendors and revenue-cycle BPOs already exist (crowding not measured). Dental deal volume fell 37% in 2025.
- **Halal flag:** Low. These are healthcare operating companies, though usually LBO-financed.

#### P3. First accrual close, KPI pack and investor update for first-time owners (search fund, SBA and ETA buyers)
- **One line:** A first-time CEO buys a business whose retiring owner kept cash-basis books (or kept them in their head). The new owner must produce monthly accrual financials, KPIs and investor or lender updates, usually without a finance team.
- **Industry / deal type:** Search funds, self-funded searchers, SBA 7(a) change-of-ownership buyers, independent sponsors; spread across industries.
- **Size:** Purchase price $1M-$20M, EBITDA $0.5M-$3M. Search fund median price is about $16M and median EBITDA $2.5M ([Calder](https://caldergr.com/acquisition-search-is-getting-harder-takeaways-from-stanfords-2026-search-fund-study)); average SBA change-of-ownership loan is about $1.18M (estimate above).
- **The task:** Monthly close (accruals, inventory, deferred revenue); budget versus actual; KPI dashboard; monthly or quarterly investor letter; an annual 12-month forecast.
- **Evidence:**
  - SearchFunder member on cadence: "if they're truly limited investors, then it may be as simple as a quarterly reporting update. If they're operators, then it may be monthly." Recommended content is P&L, balance sheet and cash flow against expectations, key sales and efficiency metrics, and risks. "Once a year it makes sense to give a 12 month forecast to your investors." The post date and author were not visible in the search summary. — [SearchFunder: standards for investor updates](https://searchfunder.com/post/standards-for-investor-updates)
  - The SBA acquisition market is about 25% of 7(a) dollars, and the new SOP 50 10 8.1 (1 Oct 2026) changes equity and lender documentation rules. — [Coleman Report](https://colemanreport.com/main-street-monday-one-in-four-sba-7a-lending-dollars-finances-business-acquisitions/); [Starfield & Smith](https://starfieldsmith.com/2026/09/best-practices-change-of-ownership-transactions-under-sop-50-10-8-1-what-sba-lenders-need-to-know/)
- **What they pay today:** A bookkeeper plus an outside CPA, or a fractional CFO at $6K-$12K a month for $5M-$15M revenue ([Acuity](https://acuity.co/fractional-cfo/), vendor). Fathom Starter at $53 a month gives dashboards ([Eightx](https://eightx.co/blog/compare/reviews/fathom-for-ecommerce-review)) but no close.
- **Six tests:** a pass (monthly close; weekly bookkeeping); b pass (fractional CFO spend; investor and lender relationships at stake); c pass; d pass (the owner-CEO approves); e pass; f pass with a warning (QuickBooks plus Fathom or LivePlan cover reporting cheaply, but not the accrual close or the commentary). **Result: 6/6.**
- **AI over 24 months:** It grows as boomer exits continue (2.3M boomer-owned firms; [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/silver-tsunami-coming-for-small-businesses-jobs)). AI lowers the cost to serve, which suits these price-sensitive buyers. The risk is that QuickBooks and Xero bundle AI close assistants (inference).
- **How to reach buyers:** SearchFunder, search-fund investor networks (Stanford and IESE communities), SBA lenders (Live Oak, Huntington, First Internet per Coleman) and business brokers.
- **Count:** About 6,100 SBA change-of-ownership deals a year (estimate), about 860 search funds cumulative, 1,200-1,400 independent sponsors, and 9,586 BizBuySell deals in 2025. The Main Street tail (median $350K) mostly cannot pay.
- **Against:** A crowded market of CAS firms, bookkeepers and offshore providers. Low budgets. The owner may simply hire one staff accountant.
- **Halal flag:** Most buyers carry interest-bearing SBA or senior debt plus seller notes. The service is bookkeeping for the operating company, not arranging debt. Flag.

#### P4. Fund-level portfolio KPI collection and board/LP pack (small sponsors)
- **One line:** Small funds and sponsors chase each portfolio company monthly for financials and KPIs in inconsistent formats, then rebuild them into board and LP packs by hand.
- **Industry / deal type:** Lower-middle-market PE funds under $500M AUM, independent sponsors, family offices and search fund investor groups.
- **Size:** Funds with 3-15 portfolio companies and 1-3 deal professionals and no portfolio-ops staff (inference).
- **Task:** Monthly data requests; normalising metric definitions; filling the fund template; variance commentary; quarterly LP reporting inputs.
- **Evidence:** Monthly data arrives as Excel, PDFs and email attachments, and the "process of collecting, cleaning, and reconciling consumes the operating team's month". Definitions drift (for example, logo churn versus revenue churn). — [Planr](https://planr.com/10-ways-spreadsheets-break-pe-portfolio-reporting/) (vendor). A common fix is one KPI dictionary plus a standard template mapped from each company's close. — [WorkWise Solutions](https://workwisesolutions.org/guides/best-ai-kpi-tracking-tools-portfolio-companies.html)
- **Today and competition:** Chronograph is used by lower-middle-market firms (Summit Park, LongWater, FPE Capital) but publishes no prices. — [Chronograph/Summit Park](https://www.chronograph.pe/summit-park-implements-chronograph-gp/); [Chronograph/LongWater](https://www.chronograph.pe/longwater-selects-chronograph/); [TrustRadius](https://www.trustradius.com/compare-products/chronograph). Chronograph raised $140M+ from Sixth Street Growth (16 June 2026) for an AI product suite and private-credit monitoring. — [Seedtable](https://seedtable.com/companies/chronograph/funding-rounds/growth-2026-06). iLEVEL and Cobalt prices were not found. Portfolio monitoring is described as "the system of record, hard to reverse". — [WorkWise](https://workwisesolutions.org/guides/best-ai-kpi-tracking-tools-portfolio-companies.html)
- **Six tests:** a pass (monthly); b weak pass (associate and operating-partner time; no dollar figure sourced); c pass; d pass (managing partner or fund CFO); e pass; f provisional pass (no sub-$100 PE tool found; VC tools exist but were not priced). **Result: 6/6, with b weakly evidenced.**
- **AI over 24 months:** This one shrinks as a service opportunity. Well-funded monitoring vendors are adding AI extraction (the Chronograph raise is direct evidence), and data collection gets commoditised.
- **Reach:** ACG, independent sponsor conferences, Axial (27% of its deals were independent sponsors), and fund administrators.
- **Count:** 1,200-1,400 independent sponsors ([Lippes](https://www.lippes.com/insights/independent-sponsor-facts-seven-things-investors-and-dealmakers-need-to-know-3052)). I found no count of US PE funds under $500M.
- **Against:** Small budget per fund, fast-moving funded competitors, and sticky systems of record.
- **Halal flag:** These clients are themselves leveraged-buyout investors. This is the most direct exposure in this lens apart from P6. Flag clearly.

#### P5. Pricebook, fee-schedule and SOP standardisation with compliance monitoring across acquired sites
- **One line:** After an acquisition, each site keeps its own prices, service menus and processes. The platform must load and then maintain a standard pricebook or fee schedule, and check monthly that technicians and front desks actually use it.
- **Industry / deal type:** Home services (HVAC, plumbing, electrical, pest, landscaping), car washes (membership pricing), vet and dental (fee schedules).
- **Evidence:**
  - Vertex Service Partners completed 30 acquisitions in three years. Each was onboarded onto a pre-configured ServiceTitan "model tenant". — [Pulse.bot](https://www.pulse.bot/saas/news/vertex-achieves-remarkable-growth-sets-new-industry-standard-with-servicetitan-ccca0e3e-f588-4f6b-9f0b-01b25aa871be/) (news-summary site; treat with caution)
  - Customer quote (vendor-published, 2021): "Adding Pricebook Pro has really opened up the amount of time it takes to make adjustments to the pricebook. Instead of hours, it's minutes." Caleb Wistad, on onboarding each acquisition. — [ServiceTitan blog, 2021](https://www.servicetitan.com/blog/webinar-recap-caleb-wistad) (vendor; verbatim per search summary)
  - ServiceTitan prices per truck or technician, so PE roll-ups add seats. — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/servicetitan)
- **Cheap-tool check:** ServiceTitan Pricebook Pro and the "model tenant" are vendor features (add-on price not found), so for ServiceTitan shops this likely **fails f**. Third-party flat-rate pricebook providers exist (names known from prior knowledge, not verified, no prices captured). It passes f for verticals without such tooling (car wash, vet, independent dental) — inference.
- **Six tests:** a pass (monthly price updates as costs move; weekly compliance checks); b weak (average-ticket leakage not quantified in sources); c pass; d pass (VP Operations); e pass; f fails for ServiceTitan-based home services. **Result: 5/6 (fails f in home services).**
- **AI over 24 months:** It shrinks in home services as ServiceTitan bundles AI pricebook tools. It holds in fragmented verticals.
- **Against:** Vertical software owns this workflow and prices it into its seats.
- **Halal flag:** Low (LBO note only).

#### P6. Lender reporting and covenant compliance package
- **One line:** Leveraged acquired companies must send lenders monthly borrowing-base certificates and quarterly compliance certificates with covenant calculations, prepared by hand from messy books.
- **Industry / deal type:** LBO portfolio companies with ABL or unitranche loans; SBA acquisition borrowers (usually lighter, often annual reporting — inference).
- **Evidence:** A sample loan agreement requires a borrowing-base certificate within 20 days after each month-end, with aged A/R and A/P and inventory reports. Another sample requires a compliance certificate with the monthly financials, signed by a responsible officer, "setting forth calculations showing compliance with the financial covenants". Deadlines across samples run 15-30 days. — [Law Insider clauses](https://www.lawinsider.com/clause/financial-statements-compliance-certifications); [Law Insider](https://www.lawinsider.com/clause/asset-base-certificate-and-funding-notice)
- **Six tests:** a pass for ABL borrowers (monthly), partial for others (quarterly); b not proven (no sourced cost for waivers or defaults); c pass (controller and CFO time); d pass; e pass (calculation plus officer sign-off as the human check); f provisional pass (no borrower-side cheap tool found). **Result: 5/6 (b unproven).**
- **AI over 24 months:** Stable. The calculation is easy for AI, and accountability stays with the officer who signs.
- **Against:** A small task per company that is often absorbed into the monthly close (P1 or P3). Lenders may supply their own portals (not verified).
- **SHARIAH FLAG (high):** The work exists only to service compliance with interest-bearing loans and borrowing-base lending. Serving the operating company is different from arranging the debt, but this is the most direct link to riba in the lens. No ruling given; the end client should decide.

#### P7. Acquired-book client retention monitoring and outreach
- **One line:** In the 90-180 days after a book of clients (insurance policies, tax and audit clients, MSP contracts) changes owner, clients leave quietly because service slips during system migration. Someone must watch renewals and at-risk accounts weekly and run outreach.
- **Industry / deal type:** Insurance agency consolidators (695 deals in 2025), PE-backed CPA platforms (179 deals in 2025), MSP roll-ups (169 deals in 2025).
- **Evidence:** "An acquisition is decided in the first ninety days after close." Buyers inherit undocumented workflows inside the seller's agency management system. Two service teams on unconnected systems create backlogs and slower response, "often the first visible sign of attrition". — [COVU](https://blog.covu.com/?p=2353) (vendor). Client attrition is listed as a typical result of poor integration. — [IWINS](https://www.iwins.com/?p=23250). Recommended steps are an intro letter to every acquired account and in-person meetings with the largest clients. — [Agency Equity](https://agencyequity.com/?p=49012). Retaining a client costs about one-fifth of winning one. — [Applied Systems](https://www1.appliedsystems.com/en-us/blog/posts/insurance-customer-retention-strategies/) (vendor claim)
- **Cheap-tool check:** Applied and other agency management systems include retention dashboards by book. CRM email sequences are cheap (HubSpot and similar). **Likely fails f** for the monitoring piece; the remaining work is cleaning seller data into the buyer's system.
- **Six tests:** a pass (weekly during the window, continuous at serial acquirers); b weak (no sourced post-acquisition attrition rate); c pass; d pass (agency principal or platform COO); e partial (relationship calls need humans); f fail. **Result: 4/6.**
- **AI over 24 months:** It shrinks as agency systems and CRMs add AI outreach.
- **Against:** Bundled tools; relationship work that is hard to hand to AI; falling insurance-agency deal volume.
- **SHARIAH FLAG (medium):** Conventional insurance itself raises questions for some Shariah scholars, and the clients are LBO-funded. No ruling given.

#### P8 (near-miss). Earnout and deferred-consideration tracking
- **One line:** Many add-on deals pay part of the price later based on retained revenue or EBITDA. Someone must calculate the metric, send sellers statements and defend the numbers.
- **Evidence:** 24% of 2025 private-target deals (excluding life sciences) had an earnout, up from 22% in 2024. Earnout achievement "does not necessarily match seller expectations"; bio/pharma earnouts pay about 19 cents on the dollar. — [SRS Acquiom Deal Terms Study](https://srsacquiom.com/our-insights/deal-terms-study); [SRS Jan 2026 newsletter](https://learn.srsacquiom.com/rs/664-KLJ-520/images/January_M%26A_Newsletter_2026.pdf?version=2) (secondary write-ups; figures from different summaries)
- **Six tests:** **a fails** (measurement is mostly quarterly or annual; monthly only at serial acquirers with many open earnouts); b plausible (disputes, but no dollar figure found); c pass (lawyers and accountants); d pass; e pass; f pass. **Result: fails a.**
- **Against:** Low frequency and a legal-heavy task. **SHARIAH FLAG:** seller notes that often sit alongside earnouts are usually interest-bearing.

#### One-off integration projects (fail a, but feed recurring work)
- Data migration, phone/CRM/scheduling consolidation, payroll and benefits harmonisation, and vendor consolidation all **fail a** for a single deal. At platforms closing 2-5 add-ons a month (Apex about 60 a year; SVP about 28 a year — estimates from [CT Acquisitions](https://www.ctacquisitions.com/guides/private-equity-hvac-2026/) and [Becker's](https://www.beckersdental.com/dso-dpms/6-dsos-among-the-biggest-pe-backed-buyers-in-healthcare-2/)), they become a repeatable "integration factory". Each also feeds P1 (monthly close), P2 (credentialing) and P7 (retention).

### Inferences
- The best-shaped opportunities sit with the **operating company or platform** (P1, P2, P3), not the fund (P4). Fund-level monitoring is attracting AI-funded vendors, while platform-level mapping, credentialing and accrual close stay messy and need a human to check them.
- Across P1, P3 and P4, cheap consolidation and dashboard tools (Fathom $53-$720 a month, Reach $149-$950 a month) remove the "software" moat. Any viable offer is a checked monthly service, not a tool.
- Blind attractiveness (pain × willingness to pay × low competition × staying power): P1 7, P2 7, P3 6, P4 5, P5 5, P6 4, P7 4, P8 3.

## Q3. Who owns the pain, who approves spend, and what do they pay today?

### Takeaway
At platforms, the pain sits with the platform CFO or controller and the VP of Integration, who report to the sponsor's operating partner. The CFO or CEO approves spend, and operating partners strongly influence it. Spend today is fractional CFOs ($6K-$25K+ a month), integration hires ($200K-$285K base), FP&A suites (about $33K-$42K a year median plus implementation) and enterprise monitoring tools (prices not public).

### Cited Findings
- VP M&A Integration roles: $230K-$280K base plus bonus and equity (Dallas industrial services platform). Salary.com estimates $205K-$286K in Chicago. One role is "newly created … reporting directly to the Operating Partner, responsible for post-acquisition integration across the portfolio". A Bain-backed Milacron role reports to the CEO and is tied to the value-creation plan. — [JRG Partners posting](https://www.jrgpartners.com/jobs/vp-ma-integration-private-equity-group-chicago-il/); [Rise/Milacron](https://joinrise.co/hillenbrand/vp-integration-and-optimization-8o7j)

- FP&A software Vendr medians: Datarails $33,300, Vena $34,286, Planful $42,403 a year; implementation adds 25%-100% of year one. Jirav: no public price (G2 says "Contact Us"). — [Aleph citing Vendr](https://www.getaleph.com/answers/fpa-software-pricing-guide-2026); [G2](https://www.g2.com/compare/datarails-vs-jirav)

- Search fund CEOs report to investor boards on a monthly or quarterly cadence. — [SearchFunder](https://searchfunder.com/post/standards-for-investor-updates)

### Inferences
- Budget holder by problem: P1 platform CFO (approved within the sponsor's value-creation-plan budget); P2 CFO or VP revenue cycle; P3 owner-CEO; P4 managing partner or fund CFO; P5 VP Ops or COO; P6 CFO; P7 agency principal or COO.
- Price anchor for an AI-plus-human monthly service: below a fractional CFO ($6K-$20K a month) and above the tools ($50-$950 a month). A plausible band is $1.5K-$8K a month, depending on entity count (inference, not tested with buyers).

## Q4. Which buyers are too small for the enterprise tools and consultants?

### Takeaway
Independent sponsors (1,200-1,400), search funds (about 860 cumulative, about 48% of recent ones acquiring), SBA acquirers (about 6,100 a year) and small platforms with fewer than 10 entities fit poorly with $33K-$80K-a-year FP&A suites, quote-only monitoring platforms and $200K+ integration hires. They are well served on software by Fathom and Reach but under-served on the human-checked work.

### Cited Findings
- Independent sponsors: 1,200-1,400 groups (PitchBook via Lippes); about 75% of deals in the lower middle market at $10M-$75M enterprise value. — [Lippes](https://www.lippes.com/insights/independent-sponsor-facts-seven-things-investors-and-dealmakers-need-to-know-3052)
- Search fund 2024-25 median price about $16M and median EBITDA $2.5M. — [Calder](https://caldergr.com/acquisition-search-is-getting-harder-takeaways-from-stanfords-2026-search-fund-study)
- Mid-market FP&A typical total of $30K-$75K a year subscription plus implementation. — [Aleph](https://www.getaleph.com/answers/fpa-software-pricing-guide-2026)
- Lower-middle-market users of Chronograph exist (Summit Park, LongWater, FPE Capital), so enterprise monitoring does reach some small funds. — [Chronograph](https://www.chronograph.pe/summit-park-implements-chronograph-gp/)

### Inferences
- **Estimate:** For a $2.5M-EBITDA search fund company, a $35K-a-year FP&A suite plus about $17K implementation (50%) is about 2% of EBITDA. That is affordable but rarely chosen without finance staff to run it. The constraint is staff, not software cost (inference).
- Family offices doing direct deals likely face the same gap, but I found no data on them.

### Gaps
- I found no count of US PE funds under $500M AUM and no family-office direct-deal counts.
- UK and Australia (EOT exits, UK accountancy consolidation, AU MSP and accounting roll-ups) were not researched because the search budget was exhausted. US-first coverage only.
